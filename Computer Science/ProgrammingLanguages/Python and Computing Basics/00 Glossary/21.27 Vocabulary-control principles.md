# 21.27 Vocabulary-control principles

1. A term is introduced only after its prerequisite ideas are available, except where an explicit forward-reference flag gives a minimal working definition.
2. A definition states the relevant domain. “Object,” “variable,” “process,” “parameter,” and “model” have different specialised meanings across chapters.
3. A metaphor does not become the definition. Boxes, recipes, conversations, and roads may aid an initial image but cannot override the stated mechanics.
4. A Python language rule is not silently replaced by a CPython observation. Implementation terms are marked and version-scoped.
5. Mathematical notation is introduced beside a prose reading and a worked application.
6. A revised model names the earlier simplification and states exactly what the refinement changes.
7. The canonical index records synonyms, abbreviations, and oppositions as cross-references rather than multiplying incompatible definitions.
