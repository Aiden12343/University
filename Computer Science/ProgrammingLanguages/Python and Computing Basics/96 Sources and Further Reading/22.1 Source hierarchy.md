# 22.1 Source hierarchy

This treatise distinguishes kinds of authority rather than treating every citation as interchangeable.

1. A **language specification** states portable Python syntax and semantics. It outranks observations about one CPython build when the question is what Python guarantees.
2. An **implementation source or manual** explains CPython behaviour. It does not automatically govern PyPy, GraalPy, MicroPython, or future CPython releases.
3. A **standard** or **request for comments** defines an interoperable protocol or format within its scope. Application conventions and deployed deviations must be identified separately.
4. A **peer-reviewed research article** presents an argument, method, and evidence evaluated through scholarly review. Review improves scrutiny but does not make findings timeless or universal.
5. A **textbook or monograph** synthesises a field and provides terminology, derivations, and exercises. Its simplifications must be checked against specifications where exact operational behaviour matters.
6. **Official library documentation** describes a versioned interface. It can contain defects and cannot specify every performance result.
7. An **empirical experiment** establishes observed behaviour for recorded conditions. It cannot by itself establish a language-wide or platform-wide guarantee.

Later editions of this work cite exact versions, sections, and archival identifiers at the paragraph level. The present bibliographic notes establish the foundational source graph and direct the reader to primary material.
