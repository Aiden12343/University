# 22.17 Bibliographic note to Branch D

Saltzer and Schroeder’s 1975 paper states enduring protection-design principles including least privilege, fail-safe defaults, complete mediation, economy of mechanism, and separation of privilege. Anderson’s *Security Engineering* integrates protocols, economics, people, and systems. Ferguson, Schneier, and Kohno explain applied cryptographic engineering; Boneh and Shoup provide a rigorous freely available modern cryptography text.

NIST publications provide standards and guidance on digital identity, cryptography, secure development, incident response, and container security. OWASP’s Application Security Verification Standard and cheat sheets translate common application controls into reviewable requirements. The MITRE Common Weakness Enumeration supplies a defect taxonomy; CVE records identify disclosed vulnerabilities but do not themselves establish deployment exploitability.

Cryptographic recommendations age. The canonical edition links algorithm and parameter choices to dated authoritative guidance rather than treating this chapter’s examples as permanent prescriptions.
