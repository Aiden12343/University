# 22.3 Bibliographic note to Chapter 1

Chapter 1’s layered treatment follows the established separation between digital abstraction, logic design, instruction-set architecture, and microarchitecture. Harris and Harris develop digital logic from transistors through processors with circuit-level examples. Patterson and Hennessy connect instruction-set design to datapaths, memory hierarchies, and performance; Hennessy and Patterson treat quantitative computer architecture at greater depth. Bryant and O’Hallaron connect machine representation to the systems programmer’s view.

The canonical historical statement of a stored-program electronic computer is von Neumann’s 1945 draft report, though the historical development involved many contributors and machines and should not be reduced to one inventor. Boolean algebra predates electronic computing; Shannon’s 1937 master’s thesis established the practical correspondence between switching circuits and symbolic logic.

Semiconductor physics, timing closure, metastability, power delivery, fabrication, and modern speculative microarchitecture exceed the first trunk chapter’s required depth and occupy the later canonical volume expansion. Authoritative processor manuals and hardware-description-language standards should be consulted for exact current architectures.
