# 22.4 Bibliographic note to Chapter 2

Operating-system concepts are developed systematically by Arpaci-Dusseau and Arpaci-Dusseau in *Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces*, which organises virtualisation, concurrency, and persistence around mechanisms and policies. Silberschatz, Galvin, and Gagne provide a broad conventional survey; Tanenbaum and Bos compare architectures and mechanisms. The POSIX standards describe portable operating-system interfaces, while Linux, Windows, and BSD documentation describe implementation-specific facilities.

Compiler structure is classically presented by Aho, Lam, Sethi, and Ullman and, with a modern implementation orientation, by Cooper and Torczon. Interpretation and compilation are not exclusive categories: language implementations can translate source to intermediate representations, interpret those representations, specialise them, and invoke ahead-of-time native libraries in one execution.
