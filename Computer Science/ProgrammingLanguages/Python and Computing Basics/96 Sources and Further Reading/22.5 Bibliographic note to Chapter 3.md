# 22.5 Bibliographic note to Chapter 3

The concepts of algorithm, computability, decidability, and Turing machines are formalised in texts such as Sipser’s *Introduction to the Theory of Computation*. The trunk deliberately gives an informal account before the reader has discrete mathematics. Later expansion introduces sets, relations, functions, proof, languages, automata, reducibility, and undecidability without implying that practical programming requires re-proving computability theory.

Abelson, Sussman, and Sussman’s *Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs* treats programs as objects of thought and develops abstraction through interpreters. Its pedagogical architecture—construct models, expose evaluation, and revise abstractions—is an important precedent for this work’s method, although the present prose and Python-specific sequence are independent.

Shell behaviour requires the manual of the selected shell and platform. POSIX specifies a portable shell language subset; Bash, PowerShell, Z shell, fish, and Windows command processors have different grammars and object/byte-stream models.
