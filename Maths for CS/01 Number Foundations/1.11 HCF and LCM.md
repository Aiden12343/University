## Introduction

In [[1.10 Factors and Multiples]], you learned to list factors and multiples of a number and to break numbers into prime factors. Now you will compare the factor and multiple structure of **two or more numbers at once**.

The **HCF** (Highest Common Factor) is the biggest number that divides all the given numbers. The **LCM** (Lowest Common Multiple) is the smallest positive number that all the given numbers divide into.

```text
HCF(12, 18) = 6
LCM(12, 18) = 36
```

This topic introduces:
- [[common factors]] — factors shared by two or more numbers
- [[highest common factor]] — the largest shared factor
- [[common multiples]] — multiples shared by two or more numbers
- [[lowest common multiple]] — the smallest shared positive multiple
- [[using prime factors]] — finding HCF or LCM by comparing prime factorisations

> [!example] Everyday idea
> If you have two repeating events — one every 6 minutes and one every 8 minutes — the LCM tells you when they happen together.

### Breadcrumb 2: Highest Common Factor

**Builds on:** [[1.11 Breadcrumb 1|Common Factors]] (HCF is the largest among them)

The [[highest common factor]] is the biggest common factor.

For 12 and 18:

```text
common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6
```

The biggest is 6.

```text
HCF(12, 18) = 6
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Find the HCF of 20 and 30.

Step 1: List factors of 20.

```text
1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20
```

Step 2: List factors of 30.

```text
1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30
```

Step 3: Identify common factors.

```text
1, 2, 5, 10
```

Step 4: Choose the highest.

```text
HCF = 10
```

Answer: `HCF(20, 30) = 10`.

### Breadcrumb 3: What HCF Is For

**Builds on:** [[1.11 Breadcrumb 2|Highest Common Factor]] (applying HCF to real problems)

HCF is useful when you need the biggest equal group size.

Example:

You have 24 red counters and 36 blue counters. You want identical packs with no leftovers.

The biggest number of packs is:

```text
HCF(24, 36) = 12
```

Each pack gets:

```text
24 / 12 = 2 red
36 / 12 = 3 blue
```

HCF is also used to simplify fractions and ratios.

```text
24/36 = 2/3
```

because both 24 and 36 divide by 12.

### Breadcrumb 5: Lowest Common Multiple

**Builds on:** [[1.11 Breadcrumb 4|Common Multiples]] (LCM is the smallest among them)

The [[lowest common multiple]] is the smallest positive common multiple.

For 4 and 6:

```text
common multiples: 12, 24, 36, ...
```

The smallest is 12.

```text
LCM(4, 6) = 12
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Find the LCM of 8 and 12.

Step 1: List multiples of 8.

```text
8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48
```

Step 2: List multiples of 12.

```text
12, 24, 36, 48
```

Step 3: Find the first shared multiple.

```text
24
```

Answer:

```text
LCM(8, 12) = 24
```

---

### Breadcrumb 6: What LCM Is For

**Builds on:** [[1.11 Breadcrumb 5|Lowest Common Multiple]] (applying LCM to scheduling problems)

LCM is useful when repeated cycles line up.

Example:

- Task A runs every 4 seconds.
- Task B runs every 6 seconds.

They both run together every:

```text
LCM(4, 6) = 12 seconds
```

> [!example] CS connection
> If one background job runs every 8 minutes and another runs every 12 minutes, the LCM tells you when they run at the same time.

### Breadcrumb 7: Prime Factor Method for HCF

**Builds on:** [[1.10 Breadcrumb 8|Prime Factorisation]] (primes are the building blocks), [[1.11 Breadcrumb 2|Highest Common Factor]] (we want shared primes, lowest powers)

Prime factors can find HCF without long factor lists.

Question: Find HCF of 36 and 60.

Step 1: Prime factorise.

```text
36 = 2 x 2 x 3 x 3 = 2^2 x 3^2
60 = 2 x 2 x 3 x 5 = 2^2 x 3 x 5
```

Step 2: Take only shared prime factors, using the lowest shared powers.

```text
shared: 2 x 2 x 3
```

Step 3: Multiply.

```text
2 x 2 x 3 = 12
```

Answer:

```text
HCF(36, 60) = 12
```

---

### Breadcrumb 8: Prime Factor Method for LCM

**Builds on:** [[1.10 Breadcrumb 8|Prime Factorisation]], [[1.11 Breadcrumb 5|Lowest Common Multiple]] (all primes needed, highest powers)

For LCM, include every prime factor needed to build both numbers, using the highest powers.

Question: Find LCM of 36 and 60.

Step 1: Prime factorise.

```text
36 = 2^2 x 3^2
60 = 2^2 x 3 x 5
```

Step 2: Take highest powers of all primes.

```text
2^2, 3^2, 5
```

Step 3: Multiply.

```text
4 x 9 x 5 = 180
```

Answer:

```text
LCM(36, 60) = 180
```

---

### Breadcrumb 9: HCF and LCM Relationship

**Builds on:** [[1.11 Breadcrumb 2|Highest Common Factor]] and [[1.11 Breadcrumb 5|Lowest Common Multiple]] (they are related by the product of the two numbers)

For two positive whole numbers:

$$
HCF(a,b) \times LCM(a,b) = a \times b
$$

Example:

```text
HCF(12, 18) = 6
LCM(12, 18) = 36
```

Check:

```text
6 x 36 = 216
12 x 18 = 216
```

This is a useful check, not the first method to learn.

---

## Why This Works

HCF looks for the prime factors two numbers share.

LCM looks for all prime factors needed so both numbers divide exactly into the result.

That is why HCF helps with reducing and grouping, while LCM helps with syncing cycles and finding common denominators.

## Worked Examples

For fully worked solutions, use [[1.11 HCF and LCM - Worked Examples]].

### Example 1 — HCF

Question: Find the highest common factor (HCF) of 16 and 24.

Step 1: List the factors of 16.

```text
1, 2, 4, 8, 16
```

Step 2: List the factors of 24.

```text
1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
```

Step 3: Identify the common factors.

```text
1, 2, 4, 8
```

Step 4: Choose the highest.

Answer: **HCF(16, 24) = 8**.

### Example 2 — LCM

Question: Find the lowest common multiple (LCM) of 5 and 6.

Step 1: List the multiples of 5.

```text
5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30
```

Step 2: List the multiples of 6.

```text
6, 12, 18, 24, 30
```

Step 3: Find the smallest shared multiple.

Answer: **LCM(5, 6) = 30**.

### Example 3 — Scheduling

Question: One event repeats every 10 seconds and another repeats every 15 seconds. After how many seconds do they happen together?

Step 1: This is an LCM problem.

```text
LCM(10, 15)
```

Step 2: Find the LCM using prime factors.

```text
10 = 2 x 5
15 = 3 x 5
Take highest powers: 2 x 3 x 5 = 30
```

Answer: **They line up every 30 seconds**.

## Common Mistakes

- Mixing up HCF and LCM.
- Listing multiples when asked for factors.
- Listing factors when asked for multiples.
- Forgetting that LCM must be a multiple of both numbers.
- Forgetting that HCF must divide both numbers.
- Choosing the largest common multiple seen so far instead of the lowest.
- In prime-factor methods, using highest powers for HCF or lowest powers for LCM.

---

## Where This Shows Up in CS

HCF and LCM appear in:

- scheduling cycles
- repeated timers
- synchronising processes
- simplifying ratios
- common denominators
- modular arithmetic
- signal periods
- memory/block alignment

Example:

```text
job_A_period = 8 minutes
job_B_period = 12 minutes
sync_time = LCM(8, 12) = 24 minutes
```

The jobs run together every 24 minutes.
