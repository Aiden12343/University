## Introduction

In [[1.5 Division and Remainders]], you learned to split quantities into equal groups and handle leftovers. A fraction is a precise way to describe that leftover part — it is a number that can sit between two whole numbers.

A fraction describes part of a whole, part of a group, or a division.

```text
1/2 means 1 out of 2 equal parts
3/4 means 3 out of 4 equal parts
```

A **mixed number** combines a whole number and a fraction, such as `1 1/2` (one and a half). An **improper fraction** has a numerator larger than the denominator, such as `7/4`.

This topic introduces:
- [[numerator and denominator]] — the numerator counts parts; the denominator names their size
- [[equivalent fractions]] — different-looking fractions with the same value
- [[simplifying]] — rewriting a fraction with smaller numbers
- [[adding fractions]] — combining fractional parts
- [[multiplying fractions]] — finding a fraction of a fraction
- [[mixed numbers]] — a whole number plus a proper fraction
- [[improper fractions]] — a fraction with numerator >= denominator

> [!example] Everyday idea
> If a pizza is cut into 4 equal slices and you eat 1 slice, you ate `1/4` of the pizza. If you eat 5 slices from two pizzas, that is `5/4` of a pizza, or `1 1/4` pizzas.

## New Words

- [[numerator and denominator]] — numerator counts parts; denominator says how many equal parts make the whole.
- [[equivalent fractions]] — different-looking fractions with the same value.
- [[simplifying]] — rewriting a fraction in an equivalent form with smaller numbers.
- [[adding fractions]] — combining fractional parts, usually by using a common denominator.
- [[multiplying fractions]] — finding a fraction of a fraction or scaling by a fractional amount.
- [[mixed numbers]] — a whole number plus a proper fraction (e.g. `2 1/3`).
- [[improper fractions]] — a fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g. `7/4`).

### Breadcrumb 1: Equal Parts Matter

**Builds on:** [[1.5 Breadcrumb 1|Division as Sharing Equally]] (sharing created equal groups; fractions use equal parts)

Fractions only work cleanly when the whole is split into equal parts.

This is `1/4`:

```text
[X][ ][ ][ ]
```

There are 4 equal parts, and 1 is selected.

This is **not** a proper fourth if the pieces are different sizes:

```text
[X][    ][ ][        ]
```

> [!warning] Equal-size warning
> If the parts are not equal, counting pieces is not enough. One huge slice is not the same amount as one tiny slice.

---

### Breadcrumb 2: Numerator and Denominator

**Builds on:** [[1.6 Breadcrumb 1|Equal Parts Matter]] (we need names for the parts)

In the fraction:

$$
\frac{3}{5}
$$

the top number is the numerator:

```text
3 parts chosen
```

the bottom number is the denominator:

```text
5 equal parts in the whole
```

So:

```text
3/5 means 3 out of 5 equal parts
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Explain `2/7`.

Step 1: Read the denominator.

```text
7 equal parts make the whole
```

Step 2: Read the numerator.

```text
2 of those parts are selected
```

Answer: `2/7` means **2 out of 7 equal parts**.

---

### Breadcrumb 3: Fractions as Division

**Builds on:** [[1.5 Breadcrumb 3|Division Undoes Multiplication]] (the fraction bar is a division symbol), [[1.6 Breadcrumb 2|Numerator and Denominator]] (numerator/denominator = division)

A fraction can also mean division.

```text
3/4 means 3 divided by 4
```

Example:

```text
1 pizza shared equally between 4 people = 1/4 pizza each
```

The fraction keeps the division exact.

> [!important] Fraction bar means divide
> The line in a fraction is not decoration. It means the numerator is divided by the denominator.

---

### Breadcrumb 4: Equivalent Fractions

**Builds on:** [[1.6 Breadcrumb 2|Numerator and Denominator]] (changing the numbers without changing the value)

[[equivalent fractions]] have the same value.

Example:

```text
1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8
```

They look different, but they mark the same amount of the whole.

Visual:

```text
1/2: [X][ ]
2/4: [X][X][ ][ ]
```

Both shade half.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Make an equivalent fraction to `3/5`.

Step 1: Multiply numerator and denominator by the same number.

Use 2:

```text
3 x 2 = 6
5 x 2 = 10
```

Step 2: Write the equivalent fraction.

```text
3/5 = 6/10
```

Answer: `6/10` is equivalent to `3/5`.

> [!important] Same operation top and bottom
> To keep the value the same, multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.

---

### Breadcrumb 5: Simplifying Fractions

**Builds on:** [[1.6 Breadcrumb 4|Equivalent Fractions]] (simplifying is reverse of building equivalent fractions)

[[simplifying]] means writing an equivalent fraction using smaller numbers.

Example:

```text
6/10 = 3/5
```

because both 6 and 10 can be divided by 2.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Simplify `12/18`.

Step 1: Find a number that divides both 12 and 18.

```text
6 divides both 12 and 18
```

Step 2: Divide numerator and denominator by 6.

```text
12 / 6 = 2
18 / 6 = 3
```

Step 3: Write the simplified fraction.

```text
12/18 = 2/3
```

Answer: `12/18` simplifies to **2/3**.

---

### Breadcrumb 6: Comparing Fractions

**Builds on:** [[1.6 Breadcrumb 4|Equivalent Fractions]] (common denominator from equivalence), [[1.1 Breadcrumb 4|Bigger, Smaller, and Equal]] (comparison extends to fractions)

Fractions are easy to compare when denominators match.

```text
3/8 < 5/8
```

because both are eighths, and 3 eighths is less than 5 eighths.

When denominators differ, use equivalent fractions.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Which is larger, `2/3` or `3/5`?

Step 1: Use a common denominator.

Both 3 and 5 fit into 15.

Step 2: Rewrite each fraction.

```text
2/3 = 10/15
3/5 = 9/15
```

Step 3: Compare.

```text
10/15 > 9/15
```

Answer:

```text
2/3 > 3/5
```

---

### Breadcrumb 7: Adding Fractions

**Builds on:** [[1.3 Breadcrumb 1|Addition as Combining]] (same operation, new representation), [[1.6 Breadcrumb 4|Equivalent Fractions]] (common denominator method)

To add fractions with the same denominator, add the numerators and keep the denominator.

```text
1/5 + 2/5 = 3/5
```

Why? You are adding fifths to fifths.

> [!warning] Do not add denominators
> `1/5 + 2/5` is not `3/10`. The denominator names the size of the parts. The part size stays fifths.

#### Different Denominators
To add different denominators, first make equivalent fractions with a common denominator.

Question:

```text
1/2 + 1/3
```

Step 1: Use common denominator 6.

```text
1/2 = 3/6
1/3 = 2/6
```

Step 2: Add.

```text
3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6
```

Answer:

```text
1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6
```

---

### Breadcrumb 8: Multiplying Fractions

**Builds on:** [[1.4 Breadcrumb 1|Multiplication as Equal Groups]] (multiplying fractions scales quantities)

Multiplying fractions often means finding "a fraction of a fraction".

Example:

```text
1/2 of 1/3
```

means:

```text
1/2 x 1/3 = 1/6
```

Rule:

```text
(a/b) x (c/d) = (a x c) / (b x d)
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Calculate `2/3 x 4/5`.

Step 1: Multiply numerators.

```text
2 x 4 = 8
```

Step 2: Multiply denominators.

```text
3 x 5 = 15
```

Answer:

```text
2/3 x 4/5 = 8/15
```

---

### Breadcrumb 9: Improper Fractions

**Builds on:** [[1.6 Breadcrumb 2|Numerator and Denominator]] (numerator < denominator is proper; numerator >= denominator is improper)

An [[improper fractions|improper fraction]] has a numerator larger than or equal to the denominator.

Examples:

```text
5/4    7/3    12/5
```

These are called improper, but they are mathematically valid. They describe quantities greater than or equal to one whole.

```text
5/4 means 5 quarters = 1 whole and 1 quarter = 1 1/4
```

Visual:

```text
One whole: [X][X][X][X]
One more:  [X]
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Represent 3 pizzas cut into halves as an improper fraction.

Each pizza cut into halves makes 2 halves per pizza.

```text
3 x 2 = 6 halves
```

Answer: `6/2`.

---

### Breadcrumb 10: Mixed Numbers

**Builds on:** [[1.6 Breadcrumb 9|Improper Fractions]] (mixed numbers are the same value written differently), [[1.6 Breadcrumb 2|Numerator and Denominator]]

A [[mixed numbers|mixed number]] combines a whole number and a proper fraction.

```text
1 1/2    2 3/4    5 2/3
```

A mixed number and an improper fraction can represent the same quantity.

```text
1 1/2 = 3/2
```

because:

```text
1 whole = 2/2
plus 1/2 = 3/2
```

#### Converting Improper to Mixed
Question: Convert `11/4` to a mixed number.

Step 1: Divide numerator by denominator.

```text
11 / 4 = 2 remainder 3
```

Step 2: The quotient is the whole number.

```text
2
```

Step 3: The remainder is the new numerator.

```text
3/4
```

Answer:

```text
11/4 = 2 3/4
```

#### Converting Mixed to Improper
Question: Convert `3 1/5` to an improper fraction.

Step 1: Multiply the whole number by the denominator.

```text
3 x 5 = 15
```

Step 2: Add the numerator.

```text
15 + 1 = 16
```

Step 3: Keep the same denominator.

```text
16/5
```

Answer:

```text
3 1/5 = 16/5
```

### Breadcrumb 11: Fractions in Computer Science

**Builds on:** [[1.6 Breadcrumb 2|Numerator and Denominator]] (CS uses fractions to describe proportions)

Fractions model proportions and rates.

Examples:

- 3 out of 10 tests failed: `3/10`
- a progress bar is 7 out of 20 steps complete: `7/20`
- a probability is 1 successful outcome out of 4 equally likely outcomes: `1/4`
- a model uses 2/3 of the data for training
- a compression ratio compares compressed size to original size

> [!example] CS connection
> If 18 out of 24 tests pass, the passing fraction is `18/24`, which simplifies to `3/4`.

## Why This Works

Fractions work because the denominator defines the unit size.

In:

```text
3/8
```

the unit is one eighth. The numerator says there are 3 of those units.

Equivalent fractions work because multiplying or dividing the top and bottom by the same number changes the description, not the amount.

$$
\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 2}{2 \times 2} = \frac{2}{4}
$$

Mixed numbers and improper fractions are the same value expressed differently — the form depends on what is more convenient.

This prepares you for [[1.7 Decimals and Percentages]], where fractions are rewritten using tenths, hundredths, and percentages.

## Worked Examples

For fully worked solutions, use [[1.6 Fractions and Mixed Numbers - Worked Examples]].

### Example 1 — Identify Parts

Question: What does `4/9` mean?

Step 1: Read the denominator.

```text
9 equal parts make the whole
```

Step 2: Read the numerator.

```text
4 of those parts are selected
```

Answer: **4 out of 9 equal parts**.

### Example 2 — Simplify

Question: Simplify `8/12`.

Step 1: Find a divisor that works for both 8 and 12.

```text
4 divides both 8 and 12
```

Step 2: Divide numerator and denominator by 4.

```text
8 / 4 = 2
12 / 4 = 3
```

Answer: **`8/12 = 2/3`**.

### Example 3 — Add

Question: Calculate `2/7 + 3/7`.

Step 1: The denominators match (both are 7).

Step 2: Add the numerators.

```text
2 + 3 = 5
```

Step 3: Keep the denominator.

```text
5/7
```

Answer: **`2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7`**.

### Example 4 — Multiply

Question: Calculate `2/5 x 3/4`.

Step 1: Multiply the numerators.

```text
2 x 3 = 6
```

Step 2: Multiply the denominators.

```text
5 x 4 = 20
```

Step 3: Simplify.

```text
6/20 = 3/10
```

Answer: **`2/5 x 3/4 = 3/10`**.

### Example 5 — Mixed to Improper

Question: Convert `2 1/3` to an improper fraction.

Step 1: Multiply the whole number by the denominator.

```text
2 x 3 = 6
```

Step 2: Add the numerator.

```text
6 + 1 = 7
```

Step 3: Keep the same denominator.

```text
7/3
```

Answer: **`2 1/3 = 7/3`**.

### Example 6 — Improper to Mixed

Question: Convert `17/5` to a mixed number.

Step 1: Divide the numerator by the denominator.

```text
17 / 5 = 3 remainder 2
```

Step 2: The quotient is the whole number.

```text
3
```

Step 3: The remainder is the new numerator.

```text
2/5
```

Answer: **`17/5 = 3 2/5`**.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating unequal pieces as valid fraction parts.
- Confusing numerator and denominator.
- Adding denominators when adding fractions.
- Simplifying only the numerator or only the denominator.
- Thinking a larger denominator always means a larger fraction. For unit fractions, `1/8` is smaller than `1/4`.
- Rounding fractions too early when exact answers are needed.
- Thinking an improper fraction is "wrong" — it is a valid mathematical form.
- Writing `2 1/2` as `21/2` (no, it means `2 + 1/2 = 5/2`).
