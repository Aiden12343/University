## Introduction

In [[1.6 Fractions and Mixed Numbers]], you learned that fractions describe parts of a whole using a numerator and denominator. Decimals and percentages are just alternative ways to write the same thing — but they use base ten, which connects back to [[1.2 Place Value and Number Lines]].

A decimal uses a point to extend place value to the right. A percentage standardises fractions to "out of 100".

```text
0.5 = 5 tenths = 1/2
0.25 = 25 hundredths = 25/100 = 1/4
50% = 50 per 100 = 1/2
```

This topic introduces:
- [[tenths and hundredths]] — parts made by splitting one whole into 10 or 100 equal parts
- [[decimal place value]] — the value of digits after the decimal point
- [[converting fractions]] — rewriting the same value as a fraction, decimal, or percentage
- [[percent means per hundred]] — percent means "out of 100"
- [[percentage increase and decrease]] — changing an amount by a percentage of the original

> [!example] Everyday idea
> If a download is 75% complete, that means 75 out of every 100 equal parts are complete. As a decimal, that is 0.75.

## Breadcrumbs

### Breadcrumb 1: Decimal Place Value

**Builds on:** [[1.2 Breadcrumb 2|Position Gives Value]] (place value now extends right of the ones column)

Whole-number place value goes left from ones:

```text
Hundreds | Tens | Ones
```

Decimal place value goes right from ones:

```text
Ones | Tenths | Hundredths | Thousandths
  1  |  1/10  |   1/100    |   1/1000
```

In:

```text
3.47
```

the 3 means 3 ones, the 4 means 4 tenths, and the 7 means 7 hundredths.

```text
3.47 = 3 + 4/10 + 7/100
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: What does `0.36` mean?

Step 1: Read the first digit after the decimal point.

```text
3 tenths
```

Step 2: Read the second digit after the decimal point.

```text
6 hundredths
```

Step 3: Combine the place values.

```text
0.36 = 3/10 + 6/100 = 36/100
```

Answer: `0.36` means **36 hundredths**.

### Breadcrumb 2: Tenths and Hundredths

**Builds on:** [[1.2 Breadcrumb 3|Expanded Form]] (fractions in expanded form become decimals), [[1.6 Breadcrumb 1|Equal Parts Matter]] (tenths and hundredths are equal parts)

One whole split into 10 equal parts gives tenths.

```text
1/10 = 0.1
```

One whole split into 100 equal parts gives hundredths.

```text
1/100 = 0.01
```

Important examples:

```text
0.1 = 1/10
0.2 = 2/10
0.01 = 1/100
0.25 = 25/100
```

> [!warning] Zero after the decimal point matters
> `0.5` and `0.05` are not the same. `0.5` is five tenths. `0.05` is five hundredths.

---

### Breadcrumb 3: Comparing Decimals

**Builds on:** [[1.2 Breadcrumb 4|Comparing Large Numbers]] (compare left to right, same method), [[1.7 Breadcrumb 1|Decimal Place Value]] (extend place value through tenths and hundredths)

To compare decimals, line up the decimal points.

Question: Which is larger, `0.7` or `0.65`?

Step 1: Write both with the same number of decimal places.

```text
0.70
0.65
```

Step 2: Compare hundredths.

```text
70 hundredths > 65 hundredths
```

Answer:

```text
0.7 > 0.65
```

> [!warning] Common decimal mistake
> `0.65` is not larger than `0.7` just because 65 is larger than 7. You must compare place values.

### Breadcrumb 4: Converting Fractions to Decimals

**Builds on:** [[1.6 Breadcrumb 4|Equivalent Fractions]] (make denominator 10 or 100), [[1.7 Breadcrumb 1|Decimal Place Value]] (tenths and hundredths map to decimal digits)

Some fractions convert easily when the denominator is 10 or 100.

```text
7/10 = 0.7
23/100 = 0.23
```

Other common conversions:

```text
1/2 = 0.5
1/4 = 0.25
3/4 = 0.75
1/5 = 0.2
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Convert `3/5` to a decimal.

Step 1: Make the denominator 10.

```text
3/5 = 6/10
```

Step 2: Write tenths as a decimal.

```text
6/10 = 0.6
```

Answer:

```text
3/5 = 0.6
```

### Breadcrumb 5: Percent Means Per Hundred

**Builds on:** [[1.7 Breadcrumb 2|Tenths and Hundredths]] (percent is hundredths), [[1.6 Breadcrumb 2|Numerator and Denominator]] (percent is a fraction with denominator 100)

[[percent means per hundred]].

```text
25% = 25 out of 100 = 25/100 = 0.25
```

Useful conversions:

```text
100% = 1
50% = 0.5 = 1/2
25% = 0.25 = 1/4
10% = 0.1 = 1/10
1% = 0.01 = 1/100
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Convert `0.37` to a percentage.

Step 1: `0.37` means 37 hundredths.

```text
0.37 = 37/100
```

Step 2: Percent means out of 100.

```text
37/100 = 37%
```

Answer:

```text
0.37 = 37%
```

### Breadcrumb 6: Finding a Percentage of an Amount

**Builds on:** [[1.4 Breadcrumb 1|Multiplication as Equal Groups]] (multiplying by a fraction), [[1.7 Breadcrumb 5|Percent Means Per Hundred]] (percent as fraction)

To find a percentage of an amount, convert the percentage to a fraction or decimal, then multiply.

Question: Find 25% of 80.

Step 1: Convert 25%.

```text
25% = 1/4
```

Step 2: Find one quarter of 80.

```text
80 / 4 = 20
```

Answer: 25% of 80 is **20**.

#### Another Method

```text
25% = 0.25
0.25 x 80 = 20
```

You will practise decimal multiplication more later. For now, use friendly fractions where possible.

### Breadcrumb 7: Percentage Increase and Decrease

**Builds on:** [[1.7 Breadcrumb 6|Finding a Percentage of an Amount]] (find the percentage, then add or subtract), [[1.3 Breadcrumb 1|Addition as Combining]] (increase adds) and [[1.3 Breadcrumb 2|Subtraction as Taking Away]] (decrease subtracts)

A percentage increase adds a percentage of the original amount.

Question: Increase 60 by 10%.

Step 1: Find 10% of 60.

```text
10% = 1/10
60 / 10 = 6
```

Step 2: Add the increase.

```text
60 + 6 = 66
```

Answer: 60 increased by 10% is **66**.

A percentage decrease subtracts a percentage of the original amount.

Question: Decrease 80 by 25%.

Step 1: Find 25% of 80.

```text
25% = 1/4
80 / 4 = 20
```

Step 2: Subtract the decrease.

```text
80 - 20 = 60
```

Answer: 80 decreased by 25% is **60**.

> [!important] Original amount matters
> Percent increase and decrease are based on the original amount unless the problem says otherwise.

### Breadcrumb 8: CS Metrics and Progress

**Builds on:** [[1.7 Breadcrumb 5|Percent Means Per Hundred]] (CS systems report progress as percentages), [[1.7 Breadcrumb 6|Finding a Percentage of an Amount]] (calculating pass rates)

Computer systems often report decimals and percentages.

Examples:

- test pass rate
- memory usage
- CPU usage
- download progress
- probability score
- model accuracy
- compression ratio

#### Chunked Example
Question: 45 out of 60 tests pass. What percentage pass?

Step 1: Write the fraction.

```text
45/60
```

Step 2: Simplify.

```text
45/60 = 3/4
```

Step 3: Convert to percentage.

```text
3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
```

Answer: **75%** of the tests pass.

## Why This Works

Decimals work because they extend place value past the ones column.

Percentages work because they standardise fractions to a denominator of 100.

$$
37\% = \frac{37}{100} = 0.37
$$

This prepares you for [[8.7 Descriptive Statistics]], [[8.9 Hypothesis Testing]], and [[10.4 Floating Point and Rounding Error]].

## Worked Examples

For fully worked solutions, use [[1.7 Decimals and Percentages - Worked Examples]].

### Example 1 — Decimal Meaning

Question: What does `0.42` mean as a fraction?

Step 1: Read the digits after the decimal point.

```text
4 tenths and 2 hundredths
```

Step 2: Write as a fraction over the place value.

```text
0.42 = 42/100
```

Answer: **`0.42 = 42/100`** (42 hundredths).

### Example 2 — Fraction to Percentage

Question: Convert `3/5` to a percentage.

Step 1: Write an equivalent fraction with denominator 100.

```text
3/5 = 6/10 = 60/100
```

Step 2: Percent means out of 100.

```text
60/100 = 60%
```

Answer: **`3/5 = 60%`**.

### Example 3 — Percentage of an Amount

Question: Find 10% of 240.

Step 1: Convert 10% to a fraction.

```text
10% = 1/10
```

Step 2: Find one tenth of 240.

```text
240 / 10 = 24
```

Answer: **10% of 240 is 24**.

### Example 4 — CS Pass Rate

Question: If 18 out of 20 tests pass, what is the pass rate as a percentage?

Step 1: Write the passing fraction.

```text
18/20
```

Step 2: Convert to a percentage.

```text
18/20 = 9/10 = 90/100 = 90%
```

Answer: **The pass rate is 90%**.

## Common Mistakes

- Thinking `0.05` and `0.5` are the same.
- Comparing decimals by ignoring place value.
- Forgetting that percent means out of 100.
- Writing `25%` as `25` instead of `0.25` or `25/100`.
- Finding a percentage increase but forgetting to add it to the original.
- Finding a percentage decrease but subtracting from the wrong original amount.
- Rounding too early and losing precision.

## Where This Shows Up in CS

Decimals and percentages appear in:

- progress bars
- test coverage
- pass/fail rates
- model accuracy
- probability outputs
- CPU and memory usage
- compression ratios
- benchmark comparisons
- floating-point numbers

Example:

```text
used_memory = 6 GB
total_memory = 8 GB
usage = 6/8 = 3/4 = 75%
```

The machine is using 75% of its memory.
