## Introduction

In [[1.8 Negative Numbers]], you learned to calculate with numbers on both sides of zero. Now you will combine addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, powers, and brackets in a single expression.

Order of operations tells you which part of a mixed calculation to do first. Without an agreed order, the same expression could have different answers.

```text
3 + 4 x 2
```

If you add first: `(3 + 4) x 2 = 14`. If you multiply first: `3 + (4 x 2) = 11`. The standard rule says multiplication happens before addition, so the correct answer is 11.

This topic introduces:
- [[brackets]] — symbols such as `( )` that group part of a calculation to be done first
- [[indices]] — powers such as `2^3`, meaning repeated multiplication
- [[multiplication and division]] — operations handled before addition and subtraction
- [[addition and subtraction]] — operations handled after multiplication and division
- [[left-to-right ties]] — the rule that operations of the same priority are done left to right

> [!example] Everyday idea
> A recipe says "add 2 cups of flour and 1 cup of sugar, then multiply by 4 batches". Without brackets or order rules, you might make 12 cups of ingredients instead of the intended 8.

## New Words

- [[brackets]] — symbols such as `( )` that group part of a calculation to be done first.
- [[indices]] — powers such as `2^3`, meaning repeated multiplication.
- [[multiplication and division]] — operations that are handled before addition and subtraction, working left to right when tied.
- [[addition and subtraction]] — operations handled after multiplication and division, working left to right when tied.
- [[left-to-right ties]] — the rule that operations of the same priority are done in reading order from left to right.

## Breadcrumbs

### Breadcrumb 1: BIDMAS

**Builds on:** [[1.8 Breadcrumb 5|Adding Positive Numbers]] through [[1.8 Breadcrumb 8|Subtracting Negative Numbers]] (all four operations with negatives), [[1.4 Breadcrumb 5|Times Tables Are Stored Facts]] (multiplication/division speed)

One common memory word is BIDMAS:

```text
B - Brackets
I - Indices
D - Division
M - Multiplication
A - Addition
S - Subtraction
```

But read it carefully:

- brackets come first
- indices come next
- division and multiplication have the same priority
- addition and subtraction have the same priority
- tied operations go left to right

> [!warning] BIDMAS trap
> BIDMAS does not mean division always comes before multiplication. Division and multiplication are tied. Work left to right.

---

### Breadcrumb 2: Brackets First

**Builds on:** [[1.9 Breadcrumb 1|BIDMAS]] (B is first in the rule)

[[brackets]] tell you to calculate a grouped part first.

Question:

```text
(3 + 4) x 2
```

Step 1: Do the bracket.

```text
3 + 4 = 7
```

Step 2: Multiply.

```text
7 x 2 = 14
```

Answer:

```text
(3 + 4) x 2 = 14
```

Without brackets:

```text
3 + 4 x 2 = 11
```

So brackets can change the answer.

---

### Breadcrumb 3: Indices

**Builds on:** [[1.9 Breadcrumb 1|BIDMAS]] (I is before MDAS), [[1.4 Breadcrumb 1|Multiplication as Equal Groups]] (indices are repeated multiplication)

[[indices]] are powers.

```text
2^3 = 2 x 2 x 2 = 8
```

Indices are done before multiplication, division, addition, and subtraction.

Question:

```text
3 + 2^3
```

Step 1: Do the index.

```text
2^3 = 8
```

Step 2: Add.

```text
3 + 8 = 11
```

Answer:

```text
3 + 2^3 = 11
```

You study powers properly in [[2.9 Indices and Powers]]. For now, you only need simple square and cube examples.

---

### Breadcrumb 4: Multiplication and Division Before Addition and Subtraction

**Builds on:** [[1.9 Breadcrumb 1|BIDMAS]] (MD before AS), [[1.4 Breadcrumb 1|Multiplication as Equal Groups]] and [[1.5 Breadcrumb 1|Division as Sharing Equally]] (these operations outrank addition/subtraction)

Multiplication and division happen before addition and subtraction unless brackets say otherwise.

Question:

```text
10 - 2 x 3
```

Step 1: Multiply first.

```text
2 x 3 = 6
```

Step 2: Subtract.

```text
10 - 6 = 4
```

Answer:

```text
10 - 2 x 3 = 4
```

> [!important] Do not simply go left to right across everything
> Only tied operations go left to right. Multiplication/division outrank addition/subtraction.

---

### Breadcrumb 5: Left-to-Right Ties

**Builds on:** [[1.9 Breadcrumb 4|Multiplication and Division Before Addition and Subtraction]] (ties only matter within the same priority level)

When operations have the same priority, work from left to right.

#### Multiplication and Division Tie
Question:

```text
24 / 6 x 2
```

Division and multiplication are tied, so work left to right.

Step 1:

```text
24 / 6 = 4
```

Step 2:

```text
4 x 2 = 8
```

Answer:

```text
24 / 6 x 2 = 8
```

If you multiplied first, you would get:

```text
24 / 12 = 2
```

That is wrong for this expression because it breaks left-to-right order.

#### Addition and Subtraction Tie
Question:

```text
10 - 4 + 3
```

Addition and subtraction are tied, so work left to right.

Step 1:

```text
10 - 4 = 6
```

Step 2:

```text
6 + 3 = 9
```

Answer:

```text
10 - 4 + 3 = 9
```

### Breadcrumb 6: Negative Numbers and Brackets

**Builds on:** [[1.8 Breadcrumb 8|Subtracting Negative Numbers]] (negative signs and subtraction look similar), [[1.9 Breadcrumb 2|Brackets First]] (brackets clarify the sign)

Negative numbers often need brackets to avoid confusion.

```text
5 + (-3)
```

means add negative 3.

```text
5 + (-3) = 2
```

```text
5 - (-3)
```

means subtract negative 3.

```text
5 - (-3) = 8
```

> [!tip] Parentheses make signs readable
> Writing `5 - -3` is hard to read. Writing `5 - (-3)` makes the meaning clear.

---

### Breadcrumb 7: Expressions in Programming

**Builds on:** [[1.9 Breadcrumb 1|BIDMAS]] (languages use the same convention), [[1.9 Breadcrumb 2|Brackets First]] (brackets override defaults)

Programming languages also have expression rules.

Example:

```text
score = 10 + 2 * 5
```

Most languages evaluate multiplication before addition:

```text
2 * 5 = 10
10 + 10 = 20
```

So:

```text
score = 20
```

If the programmer wanted 60, they would need brackets:

```text
score = (10 + 2) * 5
```

## Why This Works

Order of operations is a convention that removes ambiguity.

The expression:

```text
3 + 4 x 2
```

is not a full sentence with tone or context. It needs rules. The standard rules make sure everyone, including computers, evaluates it the same way.

This prepares you for algebraic expressions in [[2.1 Variables Expressions and Equations]].

## Worked Examples

For fully worked solutions, use [[1.9 Order of Operations - Worked Examples]].

### Example 1 — Multiplication Before Addition

Question: Calculate `6 + 3 x 4`.

Step 1: Multiplication comes before addition.

```text
3 x 4 = 12
```

Step 2: Add.

```text
6 + 12 = 18
```

Answer: **18**.

### Example 2 — Brackets First

Question: Calculate `(6 + 3) x 4`.

Step 1: Do the bracket first.

```text
6 + 3 = 9
```

Step 2: Multiply.

```text
9 x 4 = 36
```

Answer: **36**.

### Example 3 — Left-to-Right Tie

Question: Calculate `30 / 5 x 2`.

Step 1: Division and multiplication are tied; work left to right.

```text
30 / 5 = 6
```

Step 2: Multiply.

```text
6 x 2 = 12
```

Answer: **12**.

### Example 4 — Negative Number

Question: Calculate `8 - (-4) x 2`.

Step 1: Multiplication before addition and subtraction.

```text
(-4) x 2 = -8
```

Step 2: Subtracting a negative moves right.

```text
8 - (-8) = 16
```

Answer: **16**.

## Common Mistakes

- Doing every operation from left to right without priority.
- Thinking division always comes before multiplication.
- Thinking addition always comes before subtraction.
- Ignoring brackets.
- Applying a power to the wrong base.
- Misreading `5 - (-3)` as `5 - 3`.
- Changing two or three steps at once and hiding an error.

## Where This Shows Up in CS

Order of operations appears in:

- programming expressions
- spreadsheet formulas
- calculators
- compiler parsing
- expression trees
- algorithm formulas
- graphics and physics calculations

Example:

```text
damage = base + bonus * multiplier
```

This does not mean:

```text
(base + bonus) * multiplier
```

unless brackets are written.
