## Introduction

A **root** asks: "What number, multiplied by itself, gives this value?" The **square root** of 49 is 7 because 7 × 7 = 49. When the answer is not a whole number, we leave it as a **surd** — an exact root form such as √2 or 3√5 — instead of rounding too early.

**Simplifying surds** extracts square factors. **Multiplying and dividing surds** combines them under one root. **Rationalising** removes a root from the denominator. Exact roots matter because rounding can break comparisons and distance calculations.

> [!example] Everyday idea
> A square room has area 50 m². The side length is √50 metres, which simplifies to 5√2 m. Rounding √50 to 7.07 too early loses exact structure needed for further algebra.

## New Words

- [[square root]] — a value that multiplied by itself gives the original number; written √a.
- [[cube root]] — a value that multiplied by itself three times gives the original number; written ∛a.
- [[surd]] — an irrational root left in exact form, such as √3 or 2√7.
- [[rationalising]] — removing a root from the denominator by multiplying top and bottom by a suitable surd.
- [[exact form]] — an answer kept in precise surd or root notation rather than a rounded decimal.

## Breadcrumbs

### Breadcrumb 1: Square Roots Reverse Squaring

**Builds on:** [[2.9 Breadcrumb 2|Squares and Cubes]] (squaring a number), [[1.4 Breadcrumb 6|Multiplication Facts]] (recall number facts)

Squaring and square rooting are inverse operations.

```text
5^2 = 25   →   √25 = 5
```

The symbol √ means the **principal** (non-negative) square root.

> [!important] Two numbers square to the same value
> `(-4)^2 = 16` and `4^2 = 16`, but √16 = 4 by convention.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Evaluate √81.

Step 1: Ask what number squares to 81.

```text
? × ? = 81
```

Step 2: Test 9.

```text
9 × 9 = 81
```

Answer: **9**.

---

### Breadcrumb 2: Perfect Squares and Simplifying Surds

**Builds on:** [[2.10 Breadcrumb 1|Square Roots Reverse Squaring]] (root of a square is whole), [[1.10 Breadcrumb 1|Common Factors]] (factor the radicand)

A [[surd]] simplifies when the number under the root has a square factor.

```text
√50 = √(25 × 2) = √25 × √2 = 5√2
```

> [!tip] Look for the largest square factor
> For √72, use 36 not 4: √72 = √(36×2) = 6√2.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Simplify √48.

Step 1: Factor 48.

```text
48 = 16 × 3
```

Step 2: Split the root.

```text
√48 = √16 × √3 = 4√3
```

Answer: **4√3** in [[exact form]].

---

### Breadcrumb 3: Multiplying and Dividing Surds

**Builds on:** [[2.10 Breadcrumb 2|Perfect Squares and Simplifying Surds]] (combining under one root), [[2.9 Breadcrumb 1|Repeated Multiplication as a Power]] (index form of roots)

For non-negative values:

```text
√a × √b = √(ab)
√a ÷ √b = √(a/b)
```

Example:

```text
√2 × √8 = √16 = 4
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Simplify √3 × √12.

Step 1: Combine under one root.

```text
√(3 × 12) = √36
```

Step 2: Evaluate.

```text
√36 = 6
```

Answer: **6**.

---

### Breadcrumb 4: Adding Surds (Like Terms Only)

**Builds on:** [[2.10 Breadcrumb 2|Perfect Squares and Simplifying Surds]] (handling surds), [[2.2 Breadcrumb 4|Collecting Like Terms to Simplify]] (same rule applies to surds)

You may add surds only when the root part matches.

```text
3√5 + 2√5 = 5√5
```

But:

```text
√2 + √3   cannot be simplified further
```

> [!warning] Common surd mistake
> √4 + √9 ≠ √(4+9). Roots do not distribute over addition.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Simplify 2√7 + 5√7.

Step 1: Both terms are multiples of √7.

Step 2: Add coefficients.

```text
(2 + 5)√7 = 7√7
```

Answer: **7√7**.

---

### Breadcrumb 5: Cube Roots

**Builds on:** [[2.9 Breadcrumb 2|Squares and Cubes]] (cubing as exponent 3), [[2.10 Breadcrumb 1|Square Roots Reverse Squaring]] (same idea, different exponent)

A [[cube root]] reverses cubing:

```text
2^3 = 8   →   ∛8 = 2
```

Example:

```text
∛54 = ∛(27 × 2) = 3∛2
```

#### Chunked Example
Question: Evaluate ∛125.

Step 1: Find a number cubed to 125.

```text
5 × 5 × 5 = 125
```

Answer: **5**.

---

### Breadcrumb 6: Rationalising a Denominator

**Builds on:** [[2.10 Breadcrumb 3|Multiplying and Dividing Surds]] (multiply numerator and denominator), [[1.6 Breadcrumb 3|Equivalent Fractions]] (multiply top and bottom by same value)

[[rationalising]] removes a surd from the bottom of a fraction.

```text
1/√2 = (1 × √2)/(√2 × √2) = √2/2
```

This keeps the value exact and makes the denominator easier to work with.

> [!example] CS connection
> Distance formulas use √. Keeping distances in exact surd form during derivation avoids accumulated rounding error until the final display step.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Rationalise `3/√5`.

Step 1: Multiply top and bottom by √5.

```text
3/√5 = (3√5)/(√5 × √5) = 3√5/5
```

Answer: **3√5/5**.

---

## Why This Works

Roots undo powers from [[2.9 Indices and Powers]]. Simplifying surds works because √(ab) = √a × √b when a, b ≥ 0.

Factorising under the root finds perfect powers you can evaluate exactly. [[rationalising]] works because multiplying numerator and denominator by the same non-zero value preserves the fraction.

## Common Mistakes

- Adding roots incorrectly: √4 + √9 = 7, not √13.
- Leaving a square factor inside the root when it could come out.
- Rounding √2 to 1.41 before finishing algebra.
- Forgetting that √a × √a = a for a ≥ 0.
- Rationalising only the numerator instead of the denominator.
- Treating √(-4) as a real number in this course stage.

> [!failure] Mistake pattern
> √(a + b) ≠ √a + √b. Always test with a = 9, b = 16: √25 = 5 but 3 + 4 = 7.

## Where This Shows Up in CS

Surds and roots appear in:

- **distance calculations** — Pythagoras uses square roots
- **vector normalisation** — dividing by length √(...)
- **numerical precision** — when to keep exact form vs float
- **graphics and physics** — diagonal lengths and magnitudes
- **machine epsilon** — floating-point cannot store most surds exactly

```python
import math
# Exact derivation first, round only for display
length = math.sqrt(2)   # stored approximately as 1.4142135623730951
```

The computer stores an approximation, not the exact surd.
