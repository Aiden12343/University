## Introduction

In [[2.1 Variables Expressions and Equations]], you learned what variables, expressions, and equations are. An expression can contain extra pieces that do not change its value but make it harder to read. **Simplifying** means rewriting an expression in a shorter, clearer form without changing what it means.

The main tool is **collecting like terms** — adding or subtracting parts that match.

A **term** is one piece of an expression. A **coefficient** is the number multiplying a variable. **Like terms** have the same variable part. **Simplifying** combines like terms. **Substitution** checks that the simplified form matches the original.

> [!example] Everyday idea
> "3 apples and 2 apples" is the same as "5 apples." You would not say "3 apples and 2 oranges is 5 apples." Apples and oranges are different kinds of object — just as `3x` and `2y` are different kinds of term.

## New Words

- [[term]] — one part of an expression, separated by plus or minus signs.
- [[coefficient]] — the number multiplying a variable in a term, such as the `4` in `4x`.
- [[like terms]] — terms that have exactly the same variable part, such as `3x` and `5x`.
- [[simplify]] — rewrite an expression in a shorter equivalent form, usually by collecting like terms.
- [[substitution]] — replace a variable with a number and calculate the result, used to check simplification.

## Breadcrumbs

### Breadcrumb 1: Terms Are the Building Blocks

**Builds on:** [[2.1 Breadcrumb 3|Expressions Have No Equals Sign]] (expressions are made of terms)

A [[term]] is one piece of an expression joined to others by `+` or `-`.

In:

```text
3x + 5 - 2y
```

there are three terms:

```text
3x    5    -2y
```

Each term is either:
- a number on its own (a constant from [[2.1 Breadcrumb 2|Constants Are Fixed Numbers]]), or
- a number times a variable, such as `3x` or `-2y`

> [!important] Plus and minus split terms
> The signs belong to the terms that follow them.

```text
7 - 4x + 2
```

Terms: `7`, `-4x`, `2`.

#### Chunked Example
Question: List the terms in `5x - 3 + 2y`.

Step 1: Split at each `+` or `-` (keep the sign with the term that follows).

```text
5x   -3   2y
```

Step 2: Name each part.

Answer: The three terms are **5x**, **-3**, and **2y**.

---

### Breadcrumb 2: The Coefficient Is the Number in Front

**Builds on:** [[2.1 Breadcrumb 1|Variables Hold the Place of a Number]] (the variable is the letter), [[1.4 Breadcrumb 2|Multiplication as Repeated Addition]] (`4x` means `4 lots of x`)

The [[coefficient]] is the number multiplying the variable part of a term.

In `4x`:

```text
4  x  x
coefficient  variable
```

If no number is written, the coefficient is 1:

```text
x  means  1x
-x means -1x
```

> [!warning] Coefficient vs constant
> In `4x + 7`, the coefficient of `x` is 4, but `7` is a constant with no variable attached. Do not call `7` a coefficient.

#### Chunked Example
Question: What is the coefficient of `x` in `6x - 2`?

Step 1: Find the term containing `x` — that is `6x`.

Step 2: Read the number in front of `x`.

Answer: The coefficient is **6**.

### Breadcrumb 3: Like Terms Can Be Combined

**Builds on:** [[1.4 Breadcrumb 1|Multiplication as Equal Groups]] (same variable = same group), [[2.1 Breadcrumb 2|Constants Are Fixed Numbers]] (constants are a separate kind of like term)

[[like terms]] have the **same variable part**.

Like terms:

```text
3x  and  5x       (both are "x terms")
-2y and  7y       (both are "y terms")
4   and  -1        (both are constant terms)
```

Not like terms:

```text
3x  and  3y       (different variables)
3x  and  3         (one has x, one does not)
```

> [!example] Object analogy
> Like terms are the same "species." You can combine `3x + 5x` because both count `x`. You cannot combine `3x + 5y` into one term — that would mix two different variables.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Which pairs are like terms?

```text
(a) 2x and 5x
(b) 3a and 3b
(c) 4 and -1
(d) 7x and 7
```

Step 1: Compare the variable part of each pair.

Step 2: Same variable (or both constant) means like terms.

Answer:
- Like terms: **(a) 2x and 5x**, **(c) 4 and -1**
- Not like terms: **(b) 3a and 3b**, **(d) 7x and 7**

### Breadcrumb 4: Collecting Like Terms to Simplify

**Builds on:** [[1.4 Breadcrumb 1|Multiplication as Equal Groups]] (`3x + 5x = 8x` counts `x` groups), [[2.2 Breadcrumb 3|Like Terms Can Be Combined]] (only same-variable terms combine)

To [[simplify]], add or subtract the coefficients of like terms.

```text
3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x = 8x
```

For several terms:

```text
4x + 2 - x + 7
= 4x - x + 2 + 7
= 3x + 9
```

> [!tip] Group like terms first
> Rewrite the expression so like terms sit next to each other. Then combine them one group at a time.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Simplify `5x + 3 - 2x + 4`.

Step 1: Group the `x` terms and the constants.

```text
5x - 2x + 3 + 4
```

Step 2: Combine the `x` terms.

```text
(5 - 2)x = 3x
```

Step 3: Combine the constants.

```text
3 + 4 = 7
```

Answer: **3x + 7**.

---

### Breadcrumb 5: Simplifying with Negative Coefficients

**Builds on:** [[1.8 Breadcrumb 7|Subtracting Positive Numbers]] (coefficients can be negative), [[2.2 Breadcrumb 4|Collecting Like Terms to Simplify]] (same method, handle signs)

When a term is subtracted, its coefficient is negative.

```text
7x - 4x = (7 - 4)x = 3x
3 - 5x + 2x = 3 + (-5 + 2)x = 3 - 3x
```

> [!failure] Common mistake pattern
> Writing `7x - 4x = 3` drops the variable. The answer is **3x**, not 3.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Simplify `6x - 9 + 2x - 1`.

Step 1: Group like terms.

```text
6x + 2x - 9 - 1
```

Step 2: Combine `x` terms.

```text
(6 + 2)x = 8x
```

Step 3: Combine constants.

```text
-9 - 1 = -10
```

Answer: **8x - 10**.

### Breadcrumb 6: Checking by Substitution

**Builds on:** [[2.1 Breadcrumb 6|Substituting a Value into an Expression]] (evaluate both expressions at the same value), [[1.3 Breadcrumb 10|Checking with Inverse Operations]] (checking habits)

After simplifying, use substitution to verify both forms give the same value.

If `5x + 3 - 2x + 4` simplifies to `3x + 7`, pick a value such as `x = 2`:

Original:

```text
5(2) + 3 - 2(2) + 4 = 10 + 3 - 4 + 4 = 13
```

Simplified:

```text
3(2) + 7 = 6 + 7 = 13
```

Both give 13, so the simplification is likely correct.

> [!important] One test value is not a proof
> Substitution checks your work on one input. It does not prove the expressions are equal for every value — but it catches most arithmetic slips.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Check whether `4x + 1 - x + 3` simplifies correctly to `3x + 4` using `x = 5`.

Step 1: Evaluate the original with `x = 5`.

```text
4(5) + 1 - 5 + 3 = 20 + 1 - 5 + 3 = 19
```

Step 2: Evaluate the simplified form with `x = 5`.

```text
3(5) + 4 = 15 + 4 = 19
```

Step 3: Compare the results.

Answer: Both give **19**, so the simplification checks out for this value.

## Why This Works

Simplifying works because of the distributive law:

$$
3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x
$$

You are counting identical objects. Three `x` objects plus five `x` objects make eight `x` objects — the same logic as adding equal groups in [[1.4 Breadcrumb 1|Multiplication as Equal Groups]].

Unlike terms cannot merge because they represent different quantities. Just as you cannot add 3 apples and 2 oranges and call the result 5 of either, you cannot combine `3x + 2y` into a single term.

## Worked Examples

For fully worked solutions, use [[2.2 Simplifying Expressions - Worked Examples]].

### Example 1 - Identify parts
Question: In `7x - 4 + 2x`, list the terms and the coefficient of `x`.

Solution: Terms: `7x`, `-4`, `2x`. The coefficients of `x` are 7 and 2.

### Example 2 - Simplify
Question: Simplify `8x + 5 - 3x + 2`.

Solution:

```text
8x - 3x + 5 + 2 = 5x + 7
```

### Example 3 - Check by substitution
Question: Verify `6x - 2 + x + 1` simplifies to `7x - 1` when `x = 3`.

Solution: Original: `6(3) - 2 + 3 + 1 = 18 - 2 + 3 + 1 = 20`. Simplified: `7(3) - 1 = 21 - 1 = 20`. ✓

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the variable after combining: `7x - 4x = 3` instead of **3x**.
- Combining unlike terms: `3x + 2y = 5xy` is wrong — unlike terms stay separate.
- Forgetting that subtraction makes the coefficient negative: in `5 - 2x`, the coefficient of `x` is **-2**, not 2.
- Not grouping terms before combining, leading to sign errors.
- Skipping the substitution check when unsure.

## Where This Shows Up in CS

Compilers and interpreters represent expressions as **expression trees**.

```text
Expression:  3x + 5x + 2

Tree:
       +
      / \
     +   2
    / \
  3x  5x
```

Simplifying merges the two `x` branches:

```text
       +
      / \
     8x  2
```

Expression simplification also appears in:

- compiler optimisation (combining redundant calculations)
- query planners (merging filter conditions)
- computer algebra systems
- shader code generation

Every time code rewrites `a + a` as `2*a`, it is collecting like terms.
