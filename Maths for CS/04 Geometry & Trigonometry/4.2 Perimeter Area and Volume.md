## Introduction

**Perimeter** is the total distance around a shape's boundary — like measuring a fence. **Area** is how much flat surface a shape covers — like paint on a wall. **Volume** is how much three-dimensional space an object fills — like water in a tank. Each measurement uses its own unit: length for perimeter, **unit squared** for area, and **unit cubed** for volume.

> [!example] Everyday idea
> Wallpapering a bedroom needs area in square metres. Putting a fence around a garden needs perimeter in metres. Filling a storage crate needs volume in cubic centimetres.

## New Words

- [[perimeter]] — the total distance around the outside edge of a flat shape.
- [[area]] — the amount of flat surface inside a shape's boundary, measured in square units.
- [[volume]] — the amount of three-dimensional space an object occupies, measured in cubic units.
- [[unit squared]] — a square unit such as cm² or m² used to measure area.
- [[unit cubed]] — a cubic unit such as cm³ or m³ used to measure volume.

## Breadcrumbs

### Breadcrumb 1: Perimeter Is the Sum of Edge Lengths

**Builds on:** [[4.1 Breadcrumb 6|Perimeter of Triangles and Quadrilaterals]] (adding side lengths), [[1.3 Breadcrumb 2|Adding Larger Numbers]] (sum sides)

The [[perimeter]] of a polygon is found by adding every side length once.

```text
Rectangle P = 2L + 2W
Square P = 4s
```

> [!important] Length units only
> Perimeter always uses ordinary length units (cm, m, km). Never write cm² or cm³.

#### Chunked Example
Question: A rectangle is 8 cm long and 5 cm wide. Find its perimeter.

P = 2(8) + 2(5) = 16 + 10 = 26.

Answer: **26 cm**.

---

### Breadcrumb 2: Area of Rectangles and Triangles

**Builds on:** [[4.1 Breadcrumb 4|Triangles Have Three Sides, Three Angles]] (right angle for height), [[1.4 Breadcrumb 2|Multiplication as Repeated Addition]] (rows × columns)

[[Area]] counts how many unit squares fit inside a shape.

```text
Rectangle: A = length × width
Triangle: A = ½ × base × height
```

The height must meet the base at a [[right angle]] — a perpendicular drop, not a slanted side.

> [!warning] Height is not any side
> Only the perpendicular height counts. The slanted edge is not the height unless the triangle is right-angled.

#### Chunked Example
Question: Find the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and height 6 cm.

A = ½ × 10 × 6 = 5 × 6 = 30.

Answer: **30 cm²**.

---

### Breadcrumb 3: Reading and Writing Unit Squared

**Builds on:** [[4.2 Breadcrumb 2|Area of Rectangles and Triangles]] (area calculation), [[2.9 Breadcrumb 1|Repeated Multiplication as a Power]] (² means squared)

A [[unit squared]] such as cm² represents a square that is 1 cm on each side. The small 2 belongs to the **unit**, not just the number. Twelve square centimetres means twelve copies of a 1 cm × 1 cm tile.

> [!example] Tile intuition
> A 20 cm × 20 cm tile covers 400 cm² because 20 rows of 20 unit squares fit inside it.

#### Chunked Example
Question: How many cm² does one 20 cm × 20 cm tile cover?

20 × 20 = 400.

Answer: **400 cm²**.

---

### Breadcrumb 4: Volume of a Cuboid

**Builds on:** [[4.2 Breadcrumb 2|Area of Rectangles and Triangles]] (area of base), [[1.4 Breadcrumb 2|Multiplication as Repeated Addition]] (three dimensions)

[[Volume]] for a cuboid counts unit cubes that fill the interior:

```text
V = length × width × height
```

Imagine stacking layers: each layer has `length × width` unit squares, and there are `height` layers.

> [!important] Cubed units for volume
> Volume answers must include a cubed unit: cm³, m³, mm³.

#### Chunked Example
Question: A box is 4 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm. Find its volume.

V = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24.

Answer: **24 cm³**.

---

### Breadcrumb 5: Unit Cubed and Discrete Counting

**Builds on:** [[4.2 Breadcrumb 4|Volume of a Cuboid]] (volume as count of unit cubes), [[1.1 Breadcrumb 1|Counting and Whole Numbers]] (counting cubes)

A [[unit cubed]] such as m³ is a cube 1 m on each edge. Counting 1 cm³ blocks in a box is the same as finding volume.

> [!tip] Capacity link
> 1 litre = 1000 cm³.

#### Chunked Example
Question: How many 1 cm³ cubes fit in a 5 cm × 2 cm × 2 cm box?

V = 5 × 2 × 2 = 20 cm³ → 20 cubes.

Answer: **20 cubes**.

---

### Breadcrumb 6: Composite Shapes by Split and Add

**Builds on:** [[4.2 Breadcrumb 2|Area of Rectangles and Triangles]] (area of parts), [[4.2 Breadcrumb 1|Perimeter Is the Sum of Edge Lengths]] (sum boundaries)

Awkward shapes can be split into rectangles or triangles. Find each part's area, then add (or subtract removed corners). This divide-and-conquer pattern appears throughout CS.

> [!example] L-shaped floor plan
> An L-shaped room is two rectangles. Find each area separately, then add.

#### Chunked Example
Question: An L-shape is a 6 cm × 4 cm rectangle with a 2 cm × 2 cm corner removed. Find the area.

Full: 6 × 4 = 24 cm². Corner: 2 × 2 = 4 cm². Result: 24 - 4 = 20.

Answer: **20 cm²**.

---

### Breadcrumb 7: Perimeter vs Area vs Volume

**Builds on:** [[4.2 Breadcrumb 4|Volume of a Cuboid]] (three dimensions), [[4.2 Breadcrumb 2|Area of Rectangles and Triangles]] (two dimensions), [[4.2 Breadcrumb 1|Perimeter Is the Sum of Edge Lengths]] (one dimension)

Always identify **what** is being measured before choosing a formula:

```text
perimeter → add edge lengths → cm, m
area      → multiply two perpendicular lengths → cm², m²
volume    → multiply three edge lengths → cm³, m³
```

> [!warning] Do not mix measurement types
> Adding perimeter to area is meaningless — like adding metres to square metres.

#### Chunked Example
Question: A 3 cm cube: find (a) area of one face, (b) total surface area, (c) volume.

One face: 3 × 3 = 9 cm². Six faces: 6 × 9 = 54 cm². Volume: 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cm³.

Answer: **9 cm², 54 cm², 27 cm³**.

## Why This Works

Perimeter adds lengths because you trace each edge once around the boundary. Area multiplies two perpendicular lengths because you tile a surface with rows and columns of unit squares. Volume multiplies three edge lengths because you stack layers of unit cubes.

These ideas build on [[1.3 Addition and Subtraction]] (summing sides) and [[1.4 Multiplication and Division]] (counting rows × columns × layers). [[4.1 Lines Angles and Triangles]] supplies the right-angle fact that makes height well-defined.

## Common Mistakes

- Writing cm instead of cm² for area (area needs squared unit).
- Writing cm² instead of cm³ for volume (volume needs cubed unit).
- Adding side lengths when the question asks for area.
- Using a slanted side as triangle height (height must be perpendicular).
- Forgetting to double both length and width for rectangle perimeter.
- Treating 1 m² as 100 cm² (1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10,000 cm²).

> [!failure] Mistake pattern
> If the unit looks wrong (cm for area, or cm² for volume), the measurement type was confused before any calculation began.

## Where This Shows Up in CS

Game engines store **axis-aligned bounding boxes** (AABB) as width, height, depth. Texture memory is a 2D area calculation:

```python
def texture_bytes(width, height, bytes_per_pixel=4):
    return width * height * bytes_per_pixel
```

A 1920×1080 RGBA texture uses 1920 × 1080 × 4 = 8,294,400 bytes. Voxel worlds count occupied cells — a discrete volume.
