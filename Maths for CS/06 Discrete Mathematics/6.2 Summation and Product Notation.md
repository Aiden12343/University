## Introduction

Sigma notation (Σ) and pi notation (Π) are compact shorthands for sums and products. Instead of writing `1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5`, you write `Σᵢ₌₁⁵ i`. Instead of `2 × 3 × 4 × 5`, you write `Πᵢ₌₂⁵ i`.

These notations are essential for expressing algorithm running times (nested loops are nested sums), defining mathematical functions compactly, and reasoning about series. Every Σ corresponds to a for-loop accumulator; every Π corresponds to a product accumulator.

Mastering this notation unlocks: closed forms for sums (replacing loops with formulas), double sums (nested loops), and compact definitions of polynomials, series expansions, and statistical formulas.

## New Words

- [[sigma notation]] — Σ (capital Greek sigma) used to write a sum compactly: Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ aᵢ = a₁ + a₂ + ... + aₙ.
- [[product notation]] — Π (capital Greek pi) used to write a product compactly: Πᵢ₌₁ⁿ aᵢ = a₁ × a₂ × ... × aₙ.
- [[index]] — the variable (usually i, j, k) that runs through the term positions.
- [[bounds]] — the lower bound (starting index value) and upper bound (ending index value).
- [[closed form]] — a simple formula that computes the sum without iterating term by term.

> [!important] You pass this topic only when you can define all five terms without looking.

## Breadcrumbs

### Breadcrumb 1: Sigma Notation Structure

**Builds on:** [[6.1 Sequences and Series]] (terms aᵢ in order, series as sum of terms)

A sum in sigma notation has three parts:

```text
 Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ  aᵢ
 ↑    ↑  ↑
 |    |  |
 |    |  expression being summed
 |    |
 |    bounds (i goes from 1 to n)
 |
 sigma symbol
```

The index i takes every integer value from the lower bound to the upper bound. For each value, you compute the expression and add it to the total.

### Chunked Example

Question: Expand Σᵢ₌₁⁴ i²

Step 1: Write i=1, i=2, i=3, i=4.

```text
i=1: 1² = 1
i=2: 2² = 4
i=3: 3² = 9
i=4: 4² = 16
```

Step 2: Add them together: 1 + 4 + 9 + 16 = 30.

Answer: **30**.

### Chunked Example

Question: Compute Σᵏ₌₂⁵ (2k+1)

Step 1: Expand term by term.

```text
k=2: 2×2+1 = 5
k=3: 2×3+1 = 7
k=4: 2×4+1 = 9
k=5: 2×5+1 = 11
```

Step 2: Sum: 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 32.

Answer: **32**.

### Breadcrumb 2: Properties of Sigma Notation

**Builds on:** [[6.2 Summation and Product Notation]] (expanding sums), [[2.2 Simplifying Expressions]] (factorising and splitting)

These properties let you break complex sums into simpler pieces.

### Property 1: Constant Factor Rule

```text
Σc·aᵢ = c·Σaᵢ
```

If every term has the same constant factor, factor it out.

### Chunked Example

Question: Σᵢ₌₁³ 4i

Step 1: Factor the 4: 4 × Σᵢ₌₁³ i

Step 2: Σᵢ₌₁³ i = 1+2+3 = 6.

Step 3: 4 × 6 = 24.

Answer: **24**.

### Property 2: Sum Splitting Rule

```text
Σ(aᵢ + bᵢ) = Σaᵢ + Σbᵢ
```

Sum of sums equals sum of each part separately.

### Chunked Example

Question: Σᵢ₌₁³ (i + i²)

Step 1: Split: Σi + Σi²

```text
Σi = 1+2+3 = 6
Σi² = 1+4+9 = 14
```

Step 2: 6 + 14 = 20.

Step 3: Check by direct: (1+1)+(2+4)+(3+9) = 2+6+12 = 20. ✓

Answer: **20**.

### Property 3: Constant Sum Rule

```text
Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ c = n×c
```

Summing a constant c exactly n times gives n×c.

### Chunked Example

Question: Σᵢ₌₁⁵ 7

Step 1: This is 7+7+7+7+7 = 5×7 = 35.

Answer: **35**.

### Breadcrumb 3: Common Closed Forms

**Builds on:** [[6.2 Summation and Product Notation]] (properties of sigma notation), [[6.1 Sequences and Series]] (arithmetic and geometric series formulas)

These formulas let you skip expanding term by term.

```text
Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ i         = n(n+1)/2          (arithmetic series)
Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ i²        = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6
Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ i³        = [n(n+1)/2]²
Σᵢ₌₀ⁿ rⁱ        = (rⁿ⁺¹-1)/(r-1)    (geometric series, r ≠ 1)
Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ c          = n×c
```

### Chunked Example

Question: Compute Σᵢ₌₁¹⁰⁰ i using the closed form.

Step 1: Use Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ i = n(n+1)/2.

```text
100 × 101 / 2 = 10100/2 = 5050
```

Step 2: Same answer as Gauss's trick from [[6.1 Sequences and Series]].

Answer: **5050**.

### Chunked Example

Question: Compute Σᵢ₌₁⁵ (2i + 3)

Step 1: Split: Σ(2i+3) = 2Σi + Σ3

```text
2Σᵢ₌₁⁵ i = 2 × 5(5+1)/2 = 2 × 15 = 30
Σᵢ₌₁⁵ 3 = 5×3 = 15
```

Step 2: 30 + 15 = 45.

Step 3: Check: (2+3)+(4+3)+(6+3)+(8+3)+(10+3) = 5+7+9+11+13 = 45. ✓

Answer: **45**.

### Breadcrumb 4: Product Notation

**Builds on:** [[6.2 Summation and Product Notation]] (parallel structure — Σ for sum, Π for product), [[2.9 Indices and Powers]] (products of repeated factors)

Product notation uses Π (capital pi) instead of Σ.

```text
Πᵢ₌₁ⁿ aᵢ = a₁ × a₂ × a₃ × ... × aₙ
```

### Chunked Example

Question: Compute Πᵏ₌₁⁴ (k+1)

Step 1: Expand: (1+1)×(2+1)×(3+1)×(4+1) = 2×3×4×5

Step 2: 2 × 3 = 6, 6 × 4 = 24, 24 × 5 = 120.

Answer: **120**.

### Chunked Example

Question: Compute Πⱼ₌₁³ j²

Step 1: Expand: 1² × 2² × 3² = 1 × 4 × 9 = 36.

Step 2: Alternatively: (1×2×3)² = 6² = 36.

Answer: **36**.

> [!tip] Product property
> Π(aᵢ × bᵢ) = (Πaᵢ) × (Πbᵢ). This is the product version of splitting. Also: Π(c) = cⁿ for a constant.

### Breadcrumb 5: Nested Summation (Double Sums)

**Builds on:** [[6.2 Summation and Product Notation]] (single sums, closed forms)

A double sum adds over two indices:

```text
Σᵢ₌₁ᵐ Σⱼ₌₁ⁿ aᵢⱼ
```

For each value of i, you run through all j values, then add up all results.

### Chunked Example

Question: Compute Σᵢ₌₁² Σⱼ₌₁³ (i + j)

Step 1: Outer i=1: Σⱼ₌₁³ (1+j) = (1+1)+(1+2)+(1+3) = 2+3+4 = 9.

Step 2: Outer i=2: Σⱼ₌₁³ (2+j) = (2+1)+(2+2)+(2+3) = 3+4+5 = 12.

Step 3: Total = 9 + 12 = 21.

Answer: **21**.

> [!example] CS connection
> Nested summation corresponds to nested loops. Each Σ is a loop. Σᵢ₌₁ᵐ Σⱼ₌₁ⁿ is a double loop with m×n iterations.

```python
total = 0
for i in range(1, m+1):
    for j in range(1, n+1):
        total += i + j
```

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Expanding and Computing a Sum

Question: Expand and compute Σᵢ₌₁⁵ (3i − 1).

Step 1: Expand term by term.
i = 1: 3×1 − 1 = 2
i = 2: 3×2 − 1 = 5
i = 3: 3×3 − 1 = 8
i = 4: 3×4 − 1 = 11
i = 5: 3×5 − 1 = 14

Step 2: Sum the terms.
2 + 5 + 8 + 11 + 14 = 40.

Step 3: Check using properties.
Σ(3i − 1) = 3Σi − Σ1 = 3×15 − 5 = 45 − 5 = 40. ✓

Answer: **40**.

### Example 2 — Using Closed Forms

Question: Compute Σᵢ₌₁¹⁰ (2i² + 3i) using closed forms.

Step 1: Split the sum.
Σ(2i² + 3i) = 2Σi² + 3Σi.

Step 2: Apply closed forms.
Σᵢ₌₁¹⁰ i = 10×11/2 = 55.
Σᵢ₌₁¹⁰ i² = 10×11×21/6 = 2310/6 = 385.

Step 3: Combine.
2 × 385 + 3 × 55 = 770 + 165 = 935.

Answer: **935**.

### Example 3 — Product Notation

Question: Compute Πₖ₌₂⁵ (k−1).

Step 1: Expand the product.
k = 2: 2−1 = 1
k = 3: 3−1 = 2
k = 4: 4−1 = 3
k = 5: 5−1 = 4

Step 2: Multiply.
1 × 2 × 3 × 4 = 24.

Answer: **24**.

### Example 4 — Double Sum (Nested Summation)

Question: Compute Σᵢ₌₁³ Σⱼ₌₁⁴ (i × j).

Step 1: Outer i = 1: Σⱼ₌₁⁴ (1×j) = 1+2+3+4 = 10.

Step 2: Outer i = 2: Σⱼ₌₁⁴ (2×j) = 2+4+6+8 = 20.

Step 3: Outer i = 3: Σⱼ₌₁⁴ (3×j) = 3+6+9+12 = 30.

Step 4: Total = 10 + 20 + 30 = 60.

Answer: **60**.

## Why This Works

Sigma and pi notation capture the entire pattern of a sum or product in a compact form. Every Σ corresponds to a for-loop accumulator in code; every Π corresponds to a product accumulator. The closed forms exist because patterned terms produce patterned sums, derivable once and reusable forever.

Just as [[6.1 Sequences and Series]] taught that aₙ captures the pattern of a sequence, Σ captures the pattern of a sum and Π captures the pattern of a product. The properties (constant factor, splitting) mirror algebraic laws for addition and multiplication.

## Common Mistakes

> [!failure] Mistake pattern: Forgetting the index starts at the lower bound
> Σᵢ₌₃⁵ i = 3+4+5 = 12, not 1+2+3+4+5 = 15. Always check the lower bound carefully.

> [!failure] Mistake pattern: Applying the constant factor rule backwards
> Σ(c × aᵢ) = c × Σaᵢ, NOT c × aᵢ without the sum. The factor is outside the sum.

> [!failure] Mistake pattern: Expanding a product as a sum
> Πᵢ₌₁³ i = 1×2×3 = 6, NOT 1+2+3 = 6. Π means multiply, not add.

> [!failure] Mistake pattern: Incorrect nesting of double sums
> Σᵢ₌₁² Σⱼ₌₁³ i = (1+1+1) + (2+2+2) = 3+6 = 9 (inner loop adds i three times for each i).

## Where This Shows Up in CS

### Algorithm Complexity

```python
# This nested loop
for i in range(n):
    for j in range(n):
        print(i, j)
# Means: Σᵢ₌₀ⁿ⁻¹ Σⱼ₌₀ⁿ⁻¹ 1 = n² iterations
```

### Other applications

- **ML cost functions**: most loss functions use Σ over training examples
- **Image processing**: convolution uses double sums
- **Probability**: expected value E[X] = Σ x·P(X=x)
- **Database queries**: GROUP BY with SUM aggregates
- **Dynamic programming**: nested loops with accumulation
