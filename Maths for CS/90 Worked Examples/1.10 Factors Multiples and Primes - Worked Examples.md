# 1.10 Factors Multiples and Primes - Worked Examples

Theory note: [[1.10 Factors Multiples and Primes]]

Use these before attempting [[1.10 Factors Multiples and Primes - Practice Questions]].

> [!tip] Worked example routine
> For factor questions, test division. For multiple questions, build by multiplication.

## Example 1 - Listing Factors
Question: List the factors of 30.

### Step 1 - Start with 1 and the number

```text
1 x 30 = 30
```

### Step 2 - Try the next whole numbers

```text
2 x 15 = 30
3 x 10 = 30
5 x 6 = 30
```

### Step 3 - Collect the factors in order

```text
1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30
```

Answer: The factors of 30 are **1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30**.

## Example 2 - Listing Multiples
Question: List the first six multiples of 9.

### Step 1 - Multiply by 1 to 6

```text
9 x 1 = 9
9 x 2 = 18
9 x 3 = 27
9 x 4 = 36
9 x 5 = 45
9 x 6 = 54
```

Answer:

```text
9, 18, 27, 36, 45, 54
```

## Example 3 - Factor or Not
Question: Is 8 a factor of 52?

### Step 1 - Divide

```text
52 / 8 = 6 r4
```

### Step 2 - Check the remainder
There is a remainder, so 8 does not divide exactly into 52.

Answer: No, 8 is not a factor of 52.

## Example 4 - Prime or Composite
Question: Is 23 prime?

### Step 1 - Test small factors
23 is not divisible by 2, 3, or 5.

### Step 2 - List the factors
The only positive factors are:

```text
1, 23
```

Answer: 23 is prime.

## Example 5 - Composite Number
Question: Is 27 prime or composite?

### Step 1 - Find a factor other than 1 and itself

```text
3 x 9 = 27
```

### Step 2 - Decide
27 has more than two factors.

Answer: 27 is composite.

## Example 6 - Prime Factorisation
Question: Find the prime factorisation of 48.

### Step 1 - Split by 2

```text
48 = 2 x 24
```

### Step 2 - Keep splitting composite factors

```text
24 = 2 x 12
12 = 2 x 6
6 = 2 x 3
```

### Step 3 - Collect primes

```text
48 = 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 x 3
```

Answer:

```text
48 = 2^4 x 3
```

## Example 7 - Divisibility Check
Question: Is 132 divisible by 3?

### Step 1 - Add the digits

```text
1 + 3 + 2 = 6
```

### Step 2 - Check the digit sum
6 is divisible by 3.

Answer: Yes, 132 is divisible by 3.

## Example 8 - CS Prime Use
Question: Why can primes matter in cryptography?

### Step 1 - State the maths fact
Large prime numbers can be multiplied together quickly.

### Step 2 - State the hard reverse problem
For very large products, finding the original prime factors can be difficult.

Answer: Some cryptographic systems use the gap between easy multiplication and hard factorisation.
