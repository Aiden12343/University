# 1.11 HCF and LCM - Worked Examples

Theory note: [[1.11 HCF and LCM]]

Use these before attempting [[1.11 HCF and LCM - Practice Questions]].

> [!tip] Worked example routine
> Before calculating, decide whether the question is about sharing/dividing or repeating/synchronising. That usually tells you HCF or LCM.

## Example 1 - HCF by Listing Factors
Question: Find HCF of 18 and 24.

### Step 1 - List factors of 18

```text
1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
```

### Step 2 - List factors of 24

```text
1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
```

### Step 3 - Find common factors

```text
1, 2, 3, 6
```

### Step 4 - Choose the highest

```text
HCF = 6
```

Answer: `HCF(18, 24) = 6`.

## Example 2 - LCM by Listing Multiples
Question: Find LCM of 6 and 10.

### Step 1 - List multiples of 6

```text
6, 12, 18, 24, 30
```

### Step 2 - List multiples of 10

```text
10, 20, 30
```

### Step 3 - Choose the first common multiple

```text
30
```

Answer: `LCM(6, 10) = 30`.

## Example 3 - Simplifying with HCF
Question: Simplify `18/24`.

### Step 1 - Find the HCF

```text
HCF(18, 24) = 6
```

### Step 2 - Divide numerator and denominator by 6

```text
18 / 6 = 3
24 / 6 = 4
```

Answer:

```text
18/24 = 3/4
```

## Example 4 - Scheduling with LCM
Question: One task runs every 8 seconds. Another runs every 12 seconds. When do they next run together?

### Step 1 - Recognise a repeated-cycle problem
Repeated cycles use LCM.

### Step 2 - Find LCM

```text
multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24
multiples of 12: 12, 24
```

Answer: They next run together after **24 seconds**.

## Example 5 - HCF by Prime Factors
Question: Find HCF of 48 and 72.

### Step 1 - Prime factorise

```text
48 = 2^4 x 3
72 = 2^3 x 3^2
```

### Step 2 - Take shared primes with lowest powers

```text
2^3 x 3
```

### Step 3 - Multiply

```text
8 x 3 = 24
```

Answer: `HCF(48, 72) = 24`.

## Example 6 - LCM by Prime Factors
Question: Find LCM of 48 and 72.

### Step 1 - Prime factorise

```text
48 = 2^4 x 3
72 = 2^3 x 3^2
```

### Step 2 - Take all primes with highest powers

```text
2^4 x 3^2
```

### Step 3 - Multiply

```text
16 x 9 = 144
```

Answer: `LCM(48, 72) = 144`.

## Example 7 - Choosing HCF or LCM
Question: You have 20 red LEDs and 30 blue LEDs. You want identical kits with no leftovers. What is the greatest number of kits?

### Step 1 - Recognise equal grouping
No leftovers and greatest number of identical kits means HCF.

### Step 2 - Find HCF

```text
HCF(20, 30) = 10
```

Answer: You can make **10 kits**.

Each kit has:

```text
20 / 10 = 2 red LEDs
30 / 10 = 3 blue LEDs
```

## Self-Check
You understand these examples when you can:

- find HCF by listing factors
- find LCM by listing multiples
- use HCF to simplify fractions
- use LCM to synchronise cycles
- use prime factorisations for both HCF and LCM
- choose the right method from a word problem
