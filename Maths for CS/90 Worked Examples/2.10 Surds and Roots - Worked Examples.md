# 2.10 Surds and Roots - Worked Examples

Theory note: [[2.10 Surds and Roots]]

Use these before attempting [[2.10 Surds and Roots - Practice Questions]].

> [!tip] Worked example routine
> Factor the number under the root first. Keep answers in exact surd form unless asked for a decimal.

## Example 1 - Evaluate a Perfect Square Root
Question: Find √121.

### Step 1 - Identify the square

```text
11 × 11 = 121
```

Answer: **11**.

## Example 2 - Simplify a Surd
Question: Simplify √72.

### Step 1 - Find the largest square factor

```text
72 = 36 × 2
```

### Step 2 - Split and simplify

```text
√72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2
```

Answer: **6√2**.

## Example 3 - Multiply Surds
Question: Simplify √2 × √18.

### Step 1 - Combine under one root

```text
√(2 × 18) = √36
```

### Step 2 - Evaluate

```text
√36 = 6
```

Answer: **6**.

## Example 4 - Add Like Surds
Question: Simplify 5√3 + 2√3 - √3.

### Step 1 - Combine coefficients

```text
(5 + 2 - 1)√3 = 6√3
```

Answer: **6√3**.

## Example 5 - Rationalise the Denominator
Question: Rationalise `4/√2`.

### Step 1 - Multiply top and bottom by √2

```text
4/√2 = (4√2)/(√2 × √2) = 4√2/2
```

### Step 2 - Simplify the fraction

```text
4√2/2 = 2√2
```

Answer: **2√2**.

## Example 6 - Spot the Mistake
Question: A learner writes √8 + √18 = √26. Correct this.

### Step 1 - Simplify each surd separately

```text
√8 = 2√2
√18 = 3√2
```

### Step 2 - Add like terms

```text
2√2 + 3√2 = 5√2
```

Answer: **5√2**, not √26. Roots do not add inside the radical.

## Example 7 - CS Application: Distance
Question: A vector has horizontal change 1 and vertical change 2. Find the exact length.

### Step 1 - Use Pythagoras

```text
length = √(1^2 + 2^2) = √(1 + 4) = √5
```

### Step 2 - Keep exact form

Do not round √5 to 2.236 yet if further algebra follows.

Answer: **√5** units in [[exact form]].

## Self-Check
You understand these examples when you can:

- simplify surds by extracting square factors
- combine like surds only
- rationalise simple denominators
- explain why √a + √b ≠ √(a+b)
- keep roots exact in geometry-style problems
