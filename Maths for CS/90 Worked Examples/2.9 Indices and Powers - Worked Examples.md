# 2.9 Indices and Powers - Worked Examples

Theory note: [[2.9 Indices and Powers]]

Use these before attempting [[2.9 Indices and Powers - Practice Questions]].

> [!tip] Worked example routine
> Expand a power when unsure. Name the index law before each simplification step.

## Example 1 - Evaluate a Power
Question: Find `3^4`.

### Step 1 - Expand

```text
3^4 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3
```

### Step 2 - Multiply step by step

```text
3 × 3 = 9
9 × 3 = 27
27 × 3 = 81
```

Answer: **81**.

## Example 2 - Square and Cube
Question: Evaluate `12^2` and `2^5`.

### Step 1 - Square

```text
12^2 = 144
```

### Step 2 - Fifth power

```text
2^5 = 32
```

Answer: **144** and **32**.

## Example 3 - Multiplying Same-Base Powers
Question: Simplify `2^3 × 2^6`.

### Step 1 - Add exponents

```text
2^(3+6) = 2^9
```

### Step 2 - Evaluate

```text
2^9 = 512
```

Answer: **2^9 = 512**.

## Example 4 - Dividing Same-Base Powers
Question: Simplify `5^8 ÷ 5^3`.

### Step 1 - Subtract exponents

```text
5^(8-3) = 5^5
```

### Step 2 - Evaluate

```text
5^5 = 3125
```

Answer: **3125**.

## Example 5 - Power of a Power
Question: Simplify `(2^4)^3`.

### Step 1 - Multiply exponents

```text
2^(4×3) = 2^12
```

### Step 2 - Evaluate

```text
2^12 = 4096
```

Answer: **4096**.

## Example 6 - Spot the Mistake
Question: A learner writes `2^3 × 2^4 = 4^7`. Explain the error.

### Step 1 - Identify the mistake

The bases must stay the same to add exponents.

### Step 2 - Correct simplification

```text
2^3 × 2^4 = 2^7 = 128
```

Answer: You cannot combine different bases into `4^7`.

## Example 7 - CS Application: Bit Patterns
Question: An ID field uses 8 bits. How many distinct IDs are possible?

### Step 1 - Model as a power of 2

```text
2^8
```

### Step 2 - Evaluate

```text
2^8 = 256
```

### Step 3 - Interpret

Valid unsigned values are 0 to 255 — 256 patterns total.

Answer: **256 distinct values**.

- apply multiply, divide, and power-of-a-power laws
- explain why bases must match
