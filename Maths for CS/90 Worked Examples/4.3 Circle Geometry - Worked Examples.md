# 4.3 Circle Geometry - Worked Examples

Theory note: [[4.3 Circle Geometry]]

Use these examples before attempting [[4.3 Circle Geometry - Practice Questions]].

> [!tip] How to use worked examples
> Cover the solution after reading each step. Try to predict the next step before reading it. If you cannot reproduce the whole solution from scratch, you are not ready for the practice sheet.

## Example 1 - Finding Radius from Diameter
Question: A circle has diameter 18 cm. Find its radius.

### Step 1 - Recall the relationship
The radius is half the diameter: r = d ÷ 2.

### Step 2 - Substitute the diameter
r = 18 ÷ 2.

### Step 3 - Compute
r = 9.

Answer: The radius is **9 cm**.

---

## Example 2 - Finding Circumference from Radius
Question: A circle has radius 10 cm. Find its circumference. Use π ≈ 3.14.

### Step 1 - Choose the formula with radius
C = 2πr (since we know r, not d).

### Step 2 - Substitute
C = 2 × 3.14 × 10.

### Step 3 - Multiply step by step
2 × 3.14 = 6.28.
6.28 × 10 = 62.8.

Answer: The circumference is **62.8 cm**.

---

## Example 3 - Finding Area from Radius
Question: A circle has radius 6 cm. Find its area. Use π ≈ 3.14.

### Step 1 - Write the area formula
A = πr².

### Step 2 - Square the radius
r² = 6² = 6 × 6 = 36.

### Step 3 - Multiply by π
A = 3.14 × 36.

### Step 4 - Break it down
3.14 × 30 = 94.2.
3.14 × 6 = 18.84.
94.2 + 18.84 = 113.04.

Answer: The area is **113.04 cm²**.

---

## Example 4 - Finding Arc Length
Question: A circle of radius 15 cm has a central angle of 120°. Find the arc length. Use π ≈ 3.14.

### Step 1 - Write the arc length formula
Arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr.

### Step 2 - Find the fraction of the full circle
θ/360 = 120/360 = 1/3.

### Step 3 - Compute 2πr
2πr = 2 × 3.14 × 15 = 94.2.

### Step 4 - Multiply by the fraction
Arc length = (1/3) × 94.2 = 94.2 ÷ 3 = 31.4.

Answer: The arc length is **31.4 cm**.

---

## Example 5 - Spot the Mistake
Question: A student says "A circle with radius 4 cm has area A = 2πr = 2 × 3.14 × 4 = 25.12 cm²." Is this correct? If not, fix it.

### Step 1 - Identify what was computed
2πr is the formula for circumference, not area.

### Step 2 - Write the correct formula
A = πr².

### Step 3 - Compute correctly
r² = 4² = 16.
A = 3.14 × 16 = 50.24.

### Step 4 - Fix the answer
The student confused circumference with area.

Answer: The correct area is **50.24 cm²**, not 25.12 cm².

> [!failure] Common confusion
> C = 2πr and A = πr² look similar. Remember: circumference is one-dimensional (cm), area is two-dimensional (cm²). The r² in area tells you it is 2D.

## Example 6 - CS Transfer: Circle Collision Detection
Question: A game has two circular objects:
- Object A: centre (3, 4), radius 5
- Object B: centre (10, 7), radius 3

Do they collide?

### Step 1 - Compute the distance between centres
Δx = 10 - 3 = 7.
Δy = 7 - 4 = 3.
d² = 7² + 3² = 49 + 9 = 58.
d = √58 ≈ 7.62.

This uses [[4.4 Pythagoras Theorem]] (the distance formula).

### Step 2 - Sum the radii
r_sum = r₁ + r₂ = 5 + 3 = 8.

### Step 3 - Compare distance with sum of radii
7.62 < 8 → distance is less than sum of radii.

Answer: Yes, they **collide** (they overlap because the distance between centres is less than the sum of their radii).

> [!tip] Optimisation
> In real game code, we compare squared distance to avoid computing √58:
> d² = 58
> (r₁ + r₂)² = 8² = 64
> 58 < 64 → collision. No square root needed.
