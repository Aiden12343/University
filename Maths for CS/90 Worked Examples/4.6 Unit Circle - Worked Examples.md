# 4.6 Unit Circle - Worked Examples

Theory note: [[4.6 Unit Circle]]

Use these examples before attempting [[4.6 Unit Circle - Practice Questions]].

## Example 1 - Coordinates from an Angle
Question: Find the coordinates of the point on the unit circle at 60°.

### Step 1 - Recall the unit circle definition
Point = (cos θ, sin θ).

### Step 2 - Know cos 60° and sin 60°
From special angles: cos 60° = 1/2, sin 60° = √3/2.

### Step 3 - Write the coordinates
(1/2, √3/2).

Answer: **(0.5, ≈0.866)**.

## Example 2 - Angle from Coordinates
Question: Find the angle (0° to 360°) for point (0, -1) on the unit circle.

### Step 1 - Locate the quadrant
x = 0, y = -1. This is on the negative y-axis.

### Step 2 - Know the standard angles
(0, 1) is 90°, (0, -1) is 270°.

Answer: The angle is **270° (3π/2 rad)**.

## Example 3 - Sine and Cosine for 135°
Question: Find sin 135° and cos 135°.

### Step 1 - Find the reference angle
135° is in Quadrant II. Reference angle = 180° - 135° = 45°.

### Step 2 - Know sin 45° and cos 45°
sin 45° = √2/2, cos 45° = √2/2.

### Step 3 - Apply quadrant signs
Quadrant II: sin positive, cos negative.
sin 135° = +√2/2, cos 135° = -√2/2.

Answer: sin 135° = **√2/2**, cos 135° = **-√2/2**.

## Example 4 - Find Quadrant from Sign
Question: sin θ is negative and cos θ is positive. Which quadrant?

### Step 1 - CAST rule
Quadrant I: all positive. Quadrant II: sin positive. Quadrant III: tan positive. Quadrant IV: cos positive.

### Step 2 - Find matching quadrant
sin negative AND cos positive → Quadrant IV.

Answer: **Quadrant IV** (between 270° and 360°).

## Example 5 - Spot the Mistake
Question: A student says "cos 150° = √3/2 because 150° has reference angle 30° and cos 30° = √3/2."

### Step 1 - Check the reference angle
150° reference = 180° - 150° = 30°. Correct.

### Step 2 - Check the quadrant
150° is in Quadrant II.

### Step 3 - Check the sign
In Quadrant II, cos is negative.

Answer: The student forgot the sign. cos 150° = **-√3/2**.

## Example 6 - CS Transfer: Wave Generation
Question: A sine wave oscillator generates samples using sin(2πft). At f = 440 Hz and t = 0.5 ms (0.0005 s), compute the sample value.

### Step 1 - Compute the argument
2πft = 2 × π × 440 × 0.0005 = 2 × π × 0.22 = 0.44π ≈ 1.382 radians.

### Step 2 - Compute sin
Using the unit circle: at about 1.382 rad ≈ 79.2°, sin ≈ 0.982.

Answer: The sample value is approximately **0.982**.
