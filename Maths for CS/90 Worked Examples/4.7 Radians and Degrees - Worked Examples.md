# 4.7 Radians and Degrees - Worked Examples

Theory note: [[4.7 Radians and Degrees]]

Use these examples before attempting [[4.7 Radians and Degrees - Practice Questions]].

## Example 1 - Degrees to Radians
Question: Convert 45° to radians.

### Step 1 - Multiply by π/180
45 × π/180.

### Step 2 - Simplify
45/180 = 1/4.

### Step 3 - Result
(1/4) × π = π/4.

Answer: 45° = **π/4 rad**.

## Example 2 - Radians to Degrees
Question: Convert 5π/6 rad to degrees.

### Step 1 - Multiply by 180/π
(5π/6) × (180/π).

### Step 2 - Cancel π
(5/6) × 180.

### Step 3 - Compute
5 × 180 ÷ 6 = 900 ÷ 6 = 150.

Answer: 5π/6 rad = **150°**.

## Example 3 - Arc Length in Radians
Question: A circle has radius 10 cm and angle 2 rad. Find the arc length.

### Step 1 - Arc length formula (radians)
s = rθ.

### Step 2 - Substitute
s = 10 × 2 = 20.

Answer: Arc length = **20 cm**.

(Compare with degrees: s = (θ/360) × 2πr = (114.6/360) × 62.8 ≈ 20 cm. Same result, longer formula.)

## Example 4 - Sector Area in Radians
Question: A circle has radius 6 cm and angle 1.5 rad. Find the sector area.

### Step 1 - Sector area formula (radians)
A = (1/2)r²θ.

### Step 2 - Substitute
A = 0.5 × 36 × 1.5 = 0.5 × 54 = 27.

Answer: Sector area = **27 cm²**.

## Example 5 - Spot the Mistake
Question: A student converts 300° to radians: 300 × 180/π = 54000/π ≈ 17189 rad. This is clearly wrong. What was the error?

### Step 1 - Check the conversion factor
The student multiplied by 180/π (degrees conversion) instead of π/180.

### Step 2 - Correct factor
Degrees → radians: multiply by π/180.

### Step 3 - Correct calculation
300 × π/180 = 300π/180 = 5π/3.

Answer: 300° = **5π/3 rad**, not 54000/π.

## Example 6 - CS Transfer: sin() Expects Radians
Question: In Python, what does `math.sin(90)` return? What should it be?

### Step 1 - Know that math.sin expects radians
math.sin(90) = sin(90 radians) ≈ sin(5156.6°) ≈ 0.894.

### Step 2 - Correct usage
sin(90°) = sin(π/2) = math.sin(math.pi/2) = 1.0.

### Step 3 - Check
math.sin(math.pi/2) = 1.0. ✓

Answer: `math.sin(90)` returns ≈ **0.894** (not 1). The correct call is `math.sin(math.pi/2)`.
