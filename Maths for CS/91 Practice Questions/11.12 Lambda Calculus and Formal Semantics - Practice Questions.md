# 11.12 Lambda Calculus and Formal Semantics - Practice Questions

Theory note: [[11.12 Lambda Calculus and Formal Semantics]]

Do these on paper first. Check [[11.12 Lambda Calculus and Formal Semantics - Answers]] only after making a serious attempt.

## Vocabulary
1. Define lambda expression.
2. Define variable binding.
3. Define beta reduction.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. Reduce (λx.λy.x)(a)(b) using β-reduction, showing each substitution step and the final result.

6. Apply one step of β-reduction to Ω = (λx.x x)(λx.x x) and explain why this term has no normal form.

7. Reduce (λy.z)Ω using both normal-order (outermost first) and applicative-order (innermost first) reduction strategies, where Ω = (λx.x x)(λx.x x). Why do the two strategies give different outcomes?

8. In the reduction (λx.x x y)(λz.z), explain each step: identify the redex, perform the substitution, and state why no α-conversion is needed.

9. A student reduces (λx.λy.x)y as follows: (λx.λy.x)y → λy.y (substituting y for x). Identify the variable capture error and write the correct reduction using α-conversion.

10. Verify that Church numeral SUCC 1 = 2, where 1 = λf.λx.f x, SUCC = λn.λf.λx.f (n f x), and 2 = λf.λx.f (f x), by reducing SUCC 1 to β-normal form.

11. The Curry-Howard correspondence states that a term of type τ in the simply typed lambda calculus is a proof of proposition τ. For the term λf.λx.f (f (x)) with type (ι→ι)→ι→ι, explain what logical principle this term proves and how the structure of the term corresponds to the proof structure.

12. Strong normalisation guarantees every well-typed STLC term terminates, which means STLC is not Turing-complete. Explain how adding the Y combinator (fixpoint operator) in the untyped λ-calculus enables recursion and restores Turing-completeness, and why the Y combinator cannot be typed in STLC.

Pass target: 85%. Definitions must be perfect.
