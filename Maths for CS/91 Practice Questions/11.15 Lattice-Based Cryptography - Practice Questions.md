## Core Skill
5. Determine whether v = (7, 3) belongs to the lattice Λ with basis b₁ = (3, 1), b₂ = (2, 2). Solve a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ = v for integer coefficients a₁, a₂.

6. Given LWE samples (a₁ = 3, b₁ = 5) and (a₂ = 7, b₂ = 2) with modulus q = 17 and n = 1, recover the secret s ∈ ℤ₁₇ assuming no errors. Then show that the two equations are inconsistent, proving that errors must be present.

7. Change b₂ in Q6 to b₂ = 6 (keeping a₂ = 7, q = 17). Does a consistent solution for s exist now? What does this reveal about the role of noise in LWE?

8. For Babai's rounding approach to CVP with basis b₁ = (3, 1), b₂ = (2, 2) and target t = (5, 2), explain why solving t = c₁b₁ + c₂b₂ gives c = (1.5, 0.25), and why rounding to nearest integers yields the lattice point v = 2b₁ + 0b₂ = (6, 2) with distance ||t − v|| = 1.

9. A student claims that the LLL algorithm can solve the Shortest Vector Problem exactly in any lattice dimension. Is this correct? If not, state what LLL actually guarantees (approximation factor 2^{(n−1)/2}) and explain why BKZ with larger block size gives better approximations at higher computational cost.

10. For the lattice basis B = [[2, 0], [1, 1]], compute det(Λ) = |det(B)| = 2. Verify that the shortest non-zero vectors (1, 1) and (1, −1) both have squared length 2, and explain why Minkowski's bound guarantees no shorter non-zero vector exists.

11. CRYSTALS-Kyber (NIST primary post-quantum KEM) is based on Module-LWE over ℤ₃₃₂₉[x]/(x²⁵⁶+1). Explain how varying the module rank k (k = 2 for Kyber-512, k = 3 for Kyber-768, k = 4 for Kyber-1024) adjusts the security level, and why ring-LWE alone would not allow this flexible trade-off.

12. LWE-based decryption can fail when the error e exceeds a certain threshold. Explain why system parameters must balance security (larger error distribution = harder LWE) against correctness (smaller error = fewer decryption failures), and why Kyber targets a failure probability ≤ 2⁻¹²⁸.
