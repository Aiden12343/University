# 11.18 Research Methods and Mathematical Writing - Practice Questions

Theory note: [[11.18 Research Methods and Mathematical Writing]]

Do these on paper first. Check [[11.18 Research Methods and Mathematical Writing - Answers]] only after making a serious attempt.

## Vocabulary
1. Define definition.
2. Define notation.
3. Define claim.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. From the observation "neural network training is slow," formulate a specific, measurable, and scope-limited research question comparing two optimisers on a standard benchmark.

6. Write a theorem statement and proof for: "If n is an odd integer, then n² is odd." Ensure every symbol is defined before use and each step is justified.

7. Prove the converse of Q6: "If n² is odd, then n is odd" using proof by contrapositive. Show that assuming n even leads to n² even.

8. In the proof from Q6, explain why the step n = 2k + 1 implies n² = 4k² + 4k + 1 follows from integer arithmetic, and why the final conclusion (n² = 2(2k² + 2k) + 1 shows n² is odd) is valid.

9. A student attempts to prove "If n² is even, then n is even" by writing: "n² is even, so n² = 2k for some k. Then n = √(2k), so n is even." Identify two logical gaps: why √(2k) may not be an integer, and why even if it were, the equation does not prove n is even.

10. Rewrite the following epsilon-delta argument correctly: a student writes "limit as x→2 of x² is 4" with no formal proof structure. Provide a complete proof with explicit δ = min(1, ε/5), bounding |x+2| < 5, and showing |x² − 4| < ε.

11. In the IMRaD paper structure, the Method section must be self-contained: every symbol defined, every assumption stated. Explain why this is critical for reproducibility, and contrast a vague method statement ("We used standard training") with a specific one ("We trained ResNet-50 on ImageNet for 90 epochs with SGD: lr=0.1, momentum=0.9, batch size=256, weight decay=1e−4, cosine LR schedule").

12. An ablation study measures the contribution of each system component by removing it one at a time. Explain why a paper that only reports positive ablation results (e.g., "removing component X reduces accuracy") but omits negative results (e.g., "removing component Y did not affect accuracy") is misleading, and how full reporting supports scientific progress and reproducibility.

Pass target: 85%. Definitions must be perfect.
