## Vocabulary
1. Define approximation ratio.
2. Define greedy algorithm.
3. Define optimal solution.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. Apply the maximal-matching 2-approximation algorithm for Vertex Cover to G = (V,E) with V = {a,b,c,d,e} and E = {(a,b),(b,c),(c,d),(d,e),(a,e),(b,d)}. Find a maximal matching and the resulting vertex cover. What is its size?

6. For the same G in Q5, find the optimal (minimum) vertex cover. Compute the approximation ratio |C|/|OPT| achieved by your solution. Is the 2-approximation guarantee satisfied?

7. Remove edge (b,d) from G to obtain G'. Re-apply the maximal-matching 2-approximation. What is the cover size, and what is the optimal cover size for G'?

8. Explain each step of the maximal-matching 2-approximation proof: (1) why the set of endpoints of a maximal matching is a vertex cover, and (2) why |OPT| ≥ |M| where M is the matching.

## Error Checking
9. A student claims: "MST doubling gives a 2-approximation for metric TSP. Since an MST is easy to compute, this algorithm always returns a tour at most twice the optimal for any TSP instance." The student is correct about metric TSP but then says: "MST doubling also works for general TSP without triangle inequality." Identify exactly which step of the proof fails without triangle inequality.

10. For the knapsack instance with capacity W = 50 and items A(value=60, weight=10), B(100, 20), C(120, 30), the 2-approximation algorithm returns max(greedy, best-single) = max(160, 120) = 160, while optimal is 220. Verify both the greedy packing order by value/weight ratio and the best-single-item check.

## CS Transfer
11. In network design, approximation algorithms for Steiner tree and facility location provide provably good solutions to NP-hard problems. Explain why an α-approximation is valuable in practice even though it does not guarantee optimality, and give one concrete application area.

12. Distinguish between a PTAS and an FPTAS. The Knapsack problem has an FPTAS — explain why the fully polynomial dependence on 1/ε matters when deploying such algorithms in real systems.
