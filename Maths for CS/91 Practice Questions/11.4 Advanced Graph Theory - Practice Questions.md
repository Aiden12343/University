## Vocabulary
1. Define matching.
2. Define flow network.
3. Define cut.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. Compute the max flow from source s to sink t in the network with edges: s→a(6), s→b(4), a→b(3), a→t(5), b→t(2). Capacities are given in parentheses. Find the max flow value and identify a minimum s-t cut.

6. Apply the Ford-Fulkerson algorithm to the network in Q5 by sending flow along s→a→b→t, then s→a→t, then s→b→t. Show the flow and residual capacities after each step. Prove that the flow is maximal.

7. Change the capacity of edge a→t from 5 to 3. Recompute the max flow value and the minimum cut. How does the bottleneck change?

8. Explain why the max-flow min-cut theorem holds for the network in Q5. Specifically, explain why the min cut capacity equals the max flow value and why no augmenting path remains in the residual graph.

## Error Checking
9. A student tries to apply Hall's theorem to a bipartite graph with X = {A,B,C,D} and Y = {1,2,3,4} where adjacency sets are A:{1,2}, B:{2,3}, C:{3,4}, D:{1,4}. The student says: "|N({A,B,C,D})| = 4 ≥ 4, so Hall's condition holds and a perfect matching exists." Identify the exact error — check a specific subset S ⊂ X that violates the condition.

10. A connected planar graph has V = 6 vertices and E = 10 edges. Using Euler's formula V − E + F = 2, compute the number of faces F. Then verify the inequality 2E ≥ 3F. Is this graph planar? If not, which forbidden minor does it contain?

## CS Transfer
11. In computer graphics, triangle meshes are planar graphs. Explain how Euler's formula V − E + F = 2 is used to verify mesh consistency, and give the modified formula when the mesh has multiple connected components.

12. The probabilistic method proves existence without explicit construction. Give a concrete example from Ramsey theory (e.g., the lower bound R(k,k) > 2^{k/2}) and explain why such non-constructive existence proofs are valuable in theoretical computer science.
