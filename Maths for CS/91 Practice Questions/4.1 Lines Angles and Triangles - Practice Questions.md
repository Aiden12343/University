## A. Vocabulary (6 questions)
1. Define [[line]] in one sentence.
2. Define [[angle]] in one sentence.
3. Define [[parallel]] in one sentence.
4. Define [[triangle]] in one sentence.
5. Define [[angle sum]] in one sentence.
6. Match each term to its description:

```text
Term:                         Description:
(i)  line segment             A. extends infinitely in one direction
(ii) ray                      B. extends infinitely in both directions
(iii) line                    C. finite length with two endpoints
```

## B. Core Skills (10 questions)
7. Classify each angle measure: 12°, 95°, 180°, 250°, 90°.

8. A [[triangle]] has two angles: 62° and 48°. Find the third angle.

9. Identify each figure:

```text
(a) A------------------------------B
(b) <------------------------------>
(c) M------------------------------>
(d) <------------------------------N
```

10. Two [[parallel]] lines are cut by a [[transversal]]. One angle is 54°. Find the corresponding angle, the alternate angle, and the co-interior angle.

11. Classify this triangle by sides and angles: sides 4 cm, 5 cm, 6 cm; angles 48°, 57°, 75°.

12. Classify this triangle by sides and angles: sides 7 cm, 7 cm, 7 cm.

13. Classify this triangle by sides and angles: sides 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm; one angle 90°.

14. Find the perimeter of a triangle with sides 12 cm, 9 cm, 15 cm.

15. Find the perimeter of a rectangle 14 cm long and 6 cm wide.

16. Draw and label an [[acute angle]], a [[right angle]], and an [[obtuse angle]].

## C. Spot the Mistake (5 questions)
17. A student writes: "A triangle has angles 80°, 60°, and 50° because 80 + 60 + 50 = 190, and that is close to 180." What is wrong?

18. A student says: "These two lines are parallel because they look like they never meet."

```text
    /--------/
   /--------/
```

What is wrong with this reasoning?

19. A student measures an angle of 30° and says: "If I make the rays longer, the angle gets bigger." Why is this wrong?

20. A student writes: "A triangle has sides 2, 2, 2 — that is scalene because all sides are different." What is the mistake?

21. A student is given a [[transversal]] cutting [[parallel]] lines and writes: "Alternate angles sum to 180°." Is this correct? If not, what is the correct rule?

## D. Mixed Problems (8 questions)
22. In a [[triangle]], angle A = 37°, angle B = 53°. Find angle C and classify the triangle by angles.

23. A triangle has a [[right angle]] and one other angle of 38°. Find the third angle.

24. Two [[parallel]] lines are cut by a [[transversal]]. An angle is 112°. Find the co-interior angle on the same side.

25. A triangle has a 90° angle and two equal sides. Classify it fully.

26. A triangle has sides 8 cm, 8 cm, 11 cm and angles 44°, 44°, 92°. Classify it fully.

27. In the diagram below, $l \parallel m$. Angle a = 73°. Find angles b, c, d, e, f, g, h.

```text
              t
              |
    l --------+------------------------>
              |  a     b
              |  d     c
    m --------+------------------------>
              |  e     f
              |  h     g
              |
```

28. A triangle has perimeter 30 cm. Two sides are 11 cm and 9 cm. Find the third side.

29. A rectangle has perimeter 48 cm and width 8 cm. Find its length. (Hint: use $P = 2l + 2w$ from [[4.2 Perimeter Area and Volume]].)

## E. CS Transfer (5 questions)
30. In a 3D mesh, explain why a [[triangle]] is guaranteed to be planar but a quadrilateral (four-sided polygon) is not.

31. A [[ray]] in a ray tracer starts at the camera at (0, 0, 0) and goes in direction (1, 0, 0). Describe the line the ray follows. What happens at x = 10?

32. A game character faces north and rotates 90° clockwise. What [[angle]] has it turned through? What direction does it now face?

33. In computer vision, the Canny edge detector finds [[line|lines]] in an image. Two detected lines meet at a corner with an angle of 87°. Classify this angle.

34. A collision detection system needs to test if a point is inside a [[triangle]]. One method sums the angles from the point to each vertex. If the sum is 360°, the point is inside. What earlier concept does this use?
