# 5.10 Proof by Contradiction and Contrapositive - Practice Questions

Theory note: [[5.10 Proof by Contradiction and Contrapositive]]

Do these on paper first. Check [[5.10 Proof by Contradiction and Contrapositive - Answers]] only after making a serious attempt.

## Vocabulary
1. Define contradiction.
2. Define contrapositive proof.
3. Define negation.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. Prove by contrapositive: If n² is even, then n is even. First state the contrapositive, then prove it directly.

6. Prove by contradiction that there is no largest even integer.

7. Prove by contradiction: If 3n + 2 is odd, then n is odd. (Hint: assume 3n + 2 is odd AND n is even, then derive a contradiction.)

8. Explain why proving "if n² is odd, then n is odd" is easier by contrapositive than by direct proof. State the contrapositive and explain why it gives a more convenient starting point.

## Error Checking
9. A student tries to prove "If n² is odd, then n is odd" by contradiction and writes: "Assume n² is odd and n is odd." Identify the error — what should the negation of P → Q be — and state the correct initial assumption.

10. A classmate claims to have proved "If n² is even, then n is even" by checking n = 2, 4, 6, 8 and verifying that each gives both n² even and n even. Explain why this is not a valid proof, and describe the correct indirect method.

## CS Transfer
11. The proof that the halting problem is undecidable uses proof by contradiction. In one sentence, state what the proof assumes and what contradiction it derives.

12. In one paragraph, explain why proof by contradiction is especially suited for impossibility results in computer science (such as "no program can decide the halting problem"), where a direct constructive proof is not possible.
