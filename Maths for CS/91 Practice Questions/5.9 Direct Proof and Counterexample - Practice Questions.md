# 5.9 Direct Proof and Counterexample - Practice Questions

Theory note: [[5.9 Direct Proof and Counterexample]]

Do these on paper first. Check [[5.9 Direct Proof and Counterexample - Answers]] only after making a serious attempt.

## Vocabulary
1. Define theorem.
2. Define assumption.
3. Define conclusion.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. Prove directly that the sum of any two odd integers is even. Write each step with a justification (definition of odd, algebra, definition of even).

6. Prove directly: if n is an even integer, then n² is divisible by 4. Show all steps.

7. Determine whether the following statement is true or false: "For all integers n, n² + n + 41 is prime." If it is false, provide a counterexample with the full calculation.

8. In a direct proof that "if n is odd, then n² is odd", explain why the first step is to write n = 2k + 1 and justify where this expression comes from (definition of odd).

## Error Checking
9. A student attempts to prove "all prime numbers are odd" by testing the primes 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 and confirming they are all odd. Identify the logical mistake and provide a counterexample that disproves the statement.

10. A classmate claims to have proved "If n is even, then n² is even" by testing n = 4 (4² = 16, even) and n = 6 (6² = 36, even). Explain why this does NOT constitute a proof, even though the statement happens to be true.

## CS Transfer
11. A developer says "My sorting algorithm works correctly on all inputs" after testing it on 50 random arrays. In one sentence, explain why this is not a proof of correctness, referencing the concept of universal statements and counterexamples.

12. In one paragraph, explain why mathematical proof (rather than testing alone) is necessary to establish that an algorithm always produces the correct output for every possible input.

Pass target: 85%. Definitions must be perfect.
