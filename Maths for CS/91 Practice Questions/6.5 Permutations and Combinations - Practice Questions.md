# 6.5 Permutations and Combinations - Practice Questions

Theory note: [[6.5 Permutations and Combinations]]

Do these on paper first. Check [[6.5 Permutations and Combinations - Answers]] only after making a serious attempt.

## Vocabulary
1. Define permutation.
2. Define combination.
3. Define factorial.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. Compute 7! and P(10,3). Then compute C(10,3) and verify that P(10,3) = C(10,3) × 3!.

6. A committee of 4 must be chosen from 12 people.
   (a) How many different committees can be formed? (Combination)
   (b) If the committee has 4 distinct officer positions (President, VP, Secretary, Treasurer), how many ways to fill them? (Permutation)

7. How many distinct arrangements can be made from all the letters in the word "MISSISSIPPI"? (M = 1, I = 4, S = 4, P = 2; total 11 letters.)

8. For each scenario, state whether to use P(n,k) or C(n,k), then compute the value:
   (a) Number of 5-card poker hands from a standard 52-card deck.
   (b) Number of ways to award 1st, 2nd, and 3rd place among 8 runners.
   (c) Number of ways to select 3 pizza toppings from 12 available.

## Error Checking
9. A student says: "Choosing 3 people from 10 for a committee gives P(10,3) = 720 ways because we are selecting 3 people." Identify the mistake (does order matter?) and give the correct value.

10. Compute C(8,3) using the formula n!/(k!(n−k)!). Then verify your answer using the symmetry property C(8,3) = C(8,5) and recompute.

## CS Transfer
11. In machine learning feature selection, choosing 3 features from 100 gives C(100,3) possible subsets. Compute C(100,3) and explain why this combinatorial explosion makes exhaustive search over all 3-feature subsets impractical.

12. Explain the relationship P(n,k) = C(n,k) × k! in words. Why does dividing P(n,k) by k! give the number of unordered selections?

Pass target: 85%. Definitions must be perfect.
