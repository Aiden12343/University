## Vocabulary
1. Define posterior distribution.
2. Define credible interval.
3. Define Bayesian model.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. You want to estimate the bias θ of a coin. Starting with a uniform prior Beta(1, 1), you flip the coin 5 times and get 3 heads, 2 tails. Using conjugate prior updating, find the posterior distribution.

6. A stronger prior: Beta(2, 2) (weakly centred at 0.5). After observing 10 heads and 5 tails, find the posterior distribution and compute the posterior mode θ_MAP.

7. From Q6, the posterior mode is θ_MAP = (α_post - 1) / (α_post + β_post - 2). Compute this and compare it to the MLE (sample proportion). Explain why the MAP is closer to the prior mean than the MLE.

8. Two analysts study the same coin. Analyst A uses a strong prior Beta(100, 100) (believes coin is fair). Analyst B uses a weak prior Beta(1, 1). After 20 heads and 10 tails, find both posteriors and their MAP estimates. Explain why they differ.

## Error Checking
9. A student says: "A 95% credible interval [a, b] means that 95% of repeated samples will contain the true parameter." Identify the mistake and give the correct Bayesian interpretation.

10. Perform sequential Bayesian updating: prior Beta(2, 2). Day 1: 3 heads, 2 tails → posterior? Day 2: 7 heads, 3 tails → final posterior? Verify this gives the same result as one-step updating with all 10 heads and 5 tails.

## CS Transfer
11. In Thompson sampling for reinforcement learning, the algorithm draws a sample from the posterior distribution for each action and picks the action with the best sample. Explain why this naturally balances exploration and exploitation.

12. Explain why Bayesian methods provide natural regularisation. Specifically, how does a prior (e.g., Beta(100, 100)) pull estimates toward a prior belief and why would this be useful when data is limited?
