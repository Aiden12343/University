## Vocabulary
1. Define entropy.
2. Define bit.
3. Define information content.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. A fair coin flip shows heads. Compute the information content I(heads) = log₂(1 / P(heads)). Then compute I(6) for a fair six-sided die, and explain why the die outcome carries more bits of information.

6. Compute the entropy H(X) = -Σ P(x) log₂ P(x) for a fair coin. Then compare with a biased coin where P(H) = 0.9, P(T) = 0.1. Explain why the biased coin has lower entropy.

7. True distribution P(H) = 0.9, P(T) = 0.1. Your model uses Q(H) = 0.5, Q(T) = 0.5. Compute the cross-entropy H(P, Q) = -Σ P(x) log₂ Q(x) and compare it to H(P) from Q6. What do the extra bits represent?

8. A dataset has 10 examples: 5 positive, 5 negative. Splitting on feature A gives two child nodes: left (4 pos, 1 neg) and right (1 pos, 4 neg). Compute the information gain = H(parent) - (5/10)H(left) - (5/10)H(right).

## Error Checking
9. A student says: "KL divergence is symmetric, so D_KL(P‖Q) = D_KL(Q‖P) always." Identify the mistake and compute a counterexample using P = [0.9, 0.1] and Q = [0.5, 0.5].

10. Verify the relationship H(P, Q) = H(P) + D_KL(P‖Q) using the numbers from Q6 and Q7: H(P) = 0.469, H(P, Q) = 1.0. Compute D_KL(P‖Q) directly and check that it equals 1.0 - 0.469.

## CS Transfer
11. In machine learning classification, cross-entropy is commonly used as the loss function. Explain why minimising cross-entropy is equivalent to minimising the KL divergence between the true distribution and the predicted distribution.

12. Explain why mutual information I(X;Y) = 0 when X and Y are independent, and why this property makes mutual information useful for feature selection in machine learning.
