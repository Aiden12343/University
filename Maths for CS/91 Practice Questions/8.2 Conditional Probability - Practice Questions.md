## Vocabulary
1. Define conditional probability.
2. Define given.
3. Define joint probability.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. In a class of 30 students, 18 study maths, 12 study physics, and 8 study both. What is P(physics | maths)? Show your working using the formula P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B).

6. Roll a fair six-sided die. Compute P(roll > 3 | roll is even). Use the definition of conditional probability by first identifying the restricted sample space.

7. A bag contains 4 red and 6 blue marbles. Draw two without replacement. Use the multiplication rule P(A∩B) = P(A) × P(B|A) to compute P(both red).

8. P(rain) = 0.3, P(clouds | rain) = 0.9, and P(clouds | no rain) = 0.2. Use the law of total probability to compute P(clouds).

## Error Checking
9. A student says: "The test is 95% accurate, so P(disease | positive) = 0.95." Explain why this is wrong and what quantity 0.95 actually represents in conditional probability notation.

10. Use two different methods to check the answer to Q7: (i) the multiplication rule, and (ii) counting outcomes directly (after listing the sample space of all ordered pairs without replacement).

## CS Transfer
11. In a Naive Bayes spam classifier, the assumption is that words are conditionally independent given the class. Explain why this assumption simplifies P(spam | words) and what formula would be needed if the assumption were dropped.

12. Explain why P(A|B) and P(B|A) are generally not equal, and give a concrete non-medical example where confusing the two would lead to an incorrect conclusion.
