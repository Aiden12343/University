# 8.3 Bayes Theorem - Practice Questions

Theory note: [[8.3 Bayes Theorem]]

Do these on paper first. Check [[8.3 Bayes Theorem - Answers]] only after making a serious attempt.

## Vocabulary
1. Define prior.
2. Define likelihood.
3. Define posterior.
4. Write one sentence explaining the difference between two of the new words.

## Core Skill
5. P(rain) = 0.2, P(clouds | rain) = 0.9, P(clouds | no rain) = 0.3. Use Bayes Theorem to compute P(rain | clouds).

6. A disease affects 1% of the population. A test has 95% sensitivity (P(positive | disease) = 0.95) and 95% specificity (P(negative | healthy) = 0.95). You test positive. Compute P(disease | positive).

7. From Q6, suppose you take a second independent test (same accuracy) and it also comes back positive. Use sequential Bayesian updating to compute the new P(disease | both positive).

8. Three coins: one fair (P(H)=0.5), one biased (P(H)=0.7), and one double-headed (P(H)=1). You pick one at random and flip it, getting heads. Compute P(fair | heads) using the multiple-hypothesis form of Bayes Theorem.

## Error Checking
9. A prosecutor argues: "The DNA match has probability 1 in a million if innocent. The suspect's DNA matches, so the probability of guilt is 99.9999%." Identify the exact mistake (name the fallacy) and explain why the correct probability could be as low as 50%.

10. Derive Bayes Theorem from the definition of conditional probability. Start with P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B) and the multiplication rule P(A∩B) = P(B|A) × P(A), then rearrange to show P(A|B) = P(B|A) P(A) / P(B).

## CS Transfer
11. In a Naive Bayes spam filter, the posterior P(spam | words) is proportional to P(spam) × Π P(word | spam). Explain which part is the prior, which is the likelihood, and why the denominator P(words) can be ignored when comparing "spam" vs "not spam."

12. Explain why the prior probability matters so much in medical testing — specifically, why a 95% accurate test gives only ~16% posterior probability when the disease prevalence is 1%.
