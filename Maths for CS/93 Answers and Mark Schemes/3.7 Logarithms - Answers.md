## Practice Questions - Answers

### Section A - Vocabulary
1. A [[logarithm]] is the inverse of exponentiation: it tells you the power needed to make a number from a base.
2. The [[base]] is the number raised to a power.
3. A [[power]] is the exponent that tells how many repeated multiplications are used.
4. An [[inverse operation]] undoes another operation.
5. [[log laws]] are rules for simplifying logarithms of products, quotients, and powers.
6. Logs belong after exponentials because they reverse exponential statements.

### Section C - Mistake-Spotting
19. `log_2(8)` asks `2^? = 8`, so the answer is **3**, not 16.
20. Correct conversion:

```text
log_3(81) = 4
3^4 = 81
```

21. Logs do not distribute over addition. `log_2(8 + 4)` is `log_2(12)`, not `log_2(8) + log_2(4)`.
22. `log_10(1000)` asks `10^? = 1000`, so the answer is **3**.
23. Logarithmic growth is slow because it counts repeated multiplication or repeated halving; doubling n adds about one step for base-2 logs.

## Common Wrong Answers
- Treating `log_b(a)` like multiplication.
- Reversing base and result when converting to exponential form.
- Applying log laws to addition.
- Forgetting that base-2 logs count doublings or halvings.
- Giving a decimal approximation when exact log form was requested.
