## Practice Questions - Answers

### Section A - Vocabulary
1. A [[translation]] shifts a graph horizontally or vertically without changing its shape.
2. A [[reflection]] flips a graph across an axis like a mirror image.
3. A [[stretch]] multiplies coordinates to make a graph taller, wider, or both.
4. A [[scale factor]] is the number used to multiply a coordinate during a stretch.
5. A [[transformation]] is any rule that moves, flips, or stretches a graph.
6. Outside changes affect the **y-coordinate** (vertical direction).

### Section C - Mistake-Spotting
21. `f(x - 4)` shifts **right** 4, not left. Inside `x - 4` means inputs must increase by 4 to match original outputs.
22. `f(3x)` **compresses** horizontally by factor 3 (width becomes 1/3), not stretches wider.
23. `-f(x)` reflects across the **x-axis**; `f(-x)` reflects across the **y-axis**. They differ for most functions.
24. `f(x) + 2` moves points **up** 2 (changes y), not right (which would be `f(x - 2)`).
25. `y = -2f(x)` **does** reflect across the x-axis because the multiplier is negative, as well as stretching vertically by 2.

### Section D - Mixed Practice


27.

```text

```

28. From `y = x^2`: **right 1**, **vertical stretch factor 3**, **up 4**, **reflection in x-axis**.

29. Vertex: **(-3, -5)**.

30. `(1, 4)` to `(1, 7)`: **vertical translation up 3** (`f(x) + 3`). To `(4, 4)`: **horizontal translation right 3** (`f(x - 3)`).

31. No. `(-x)^2 = x^2` for all real x, so the graphs are identical.

32. `f(0.5x)` **stretches** horizontally by factor 2 (graph becomes twice as wide).

## Common Wrong Answers
- Reversing horizontal translation direction for inside changes.
- Treating `f(bx)` as horizontal stretch when |b| > 1 (it compresses).
- Confusing `-f(x)` with `f(-x)`.
- Applying outside changes to x instead of y.
- Forgetting that negative scale factors include reflection.
