Most of the content of this course is sourced from a variety of public websites.
The example code is mostly from : https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/ which is a good foundational resource.

As a personal preference, i highly recommend the MIT OCW lecture videos and their problem sets. They are world renowned and completely free. Ana Bell specifically is a very engaging - and rather attractive - lecturer.

You can find the OCW resources at :

https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-0001-introduction-to-computer-science-and-programming-in-python-fall-2016/ 

Or alternatively search through :
https://ocw.mit.edu/search/?d=Electrical%20Engineering%20and%20Computer%20Science&q=introduction%20to%20programming&s=department_course_numbers.sort_coursenum which will give you a rather extensive amount of video content taught by a university that is...well... a bit better than NTU.

All OCW courses come with downloadable problem sets, transcription and the undergraduate work specifically, takes the assumption you are starting from zero.

They are all fast paced, but since they are just videos, you can go through them as much as you wish, pause, and take the time to understand concepts. 

A small note, to pay attention to the date the videos were recorded. Programming changes all the time, and although the basic fundamentals don't change (much!...), there are smaller aspects that will be different now vs when the videos were recorded. 

If you complete the more general programming, you can move onto subsections dedicated to aspects like algorithm design, software construction and .. wait for it .. building ACTUAL LASERS.
